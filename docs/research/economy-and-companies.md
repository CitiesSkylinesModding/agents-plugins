**Ruled (2026-08-06, the citizens-and-households pass; conflicts.md; [ADR 0004](../adr/0004-a-mechanics-reference-names-the-component-not-the-balance-value.md)).** A prefab value ships as component and field, never as a number — widened from parameter singletons to per-prefab components.
This topic is the one where that ruling bites hardest, because almost everything it owns is a number on a prefab. Three things survive it and are the spine of what ships: **C# constants ship as numbers** (`EconomyUtils.kCompanyUpdatesPerDay`, `IndustrialAISystem.kMaxVirtualResourceStorage`, `BuyingCompanySystem.kResourceLowStockAmount`, every `kUpdatesPerDay`, and the literals inside a formula); **formulas ship whole** (the production expression, the profitability byte, the attractiveness sigmoid, the tourist-target curve, the trade-cost expression); and **the map ships** — which component owns which family of numbers, with the access shape beside it.
Two consequences bite here specifically. A **derived ratio is a magnitude in disguise**: "an office company earns three times what an industrial one does per worker" is arithmetic over `ResourceData.m_Price` and `m_NeededWorkPerUnit` and does not ship — state the direction and name the fields. And a **non-numeric prefab value is still a prefab value**: `FeeParameters.m_Adjustable` decides whether a service fee is player-adjustable at all, `ResourceData.m_RequireNaturalResource` decides whether an extractor needs a deposit under it, and `ResourceData.m_IsProduceable` / `m_IsTradable` / `m_IsMaterial` / `m_IsLeisure` decide which half of the economy a resource belongs to — all ship as the field to read, with the reason to read it.

**Ruled (2026-08-08, the zoning-buildings-and-land-value pass; conflicts.md).** A field initializer on a prefab class ships as no number.
**This topic's prefab classes carry the ruled shape, and the live reads make it the strongest demonstration the pipeline has produced.** `EconomyPrefab` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/EconomyPrefab.cs:14-152`) declares 30-odd serialized fields with C# initializers and `LateInitialize` copies all of them into `EconomyParameterData` (`:165-218`); `ExtractorParameterPrefab` (`ExtractorParameterPrefab.cs:12-36`, `LateInitialize` at `:49-65`), `AttractivenessParametersPrefab` (`:12-39`, `LateInitialize` at `:52-69`) and `OutsideTradeParameterPrefab` (`:12-65`, **`Initialize` rather than `LateInitialize`**, at `:73-99`) are the same shape. Every one of those literals is compiled into the decompiled source at a citable line and is **not** the operative value: Unity deserialisation overwrites each serialized field from the shipped asset, and a mode pass can overwrite it again.
The live diff is not marginal. Read off the singleton at 1.6.0f1, `EconomyParameterData` differs from `EconomyPrefab`'s initializers in at least ten fields, several by an order of magnitude: loan interest range `(0.01, 0.10)` against an initialized `(0.02, 0.20)`; company bankruptcy limit −50,000 against −5,000,000; player start money 2,000,000 against 800,000; specialization production coefficient 1,000,000 against 10,000,000; profitability range `(−10000, 150000)` against `(−12700, 12700)`; city-service wage adjustment 1 against 0.5; relocation cost multiplier 0 against 1; tree cost multipliers `(0,0,0)` against `(2,5,10)`; residential upkeep exponent 1.05 against 1.3; land-value modifier `(0.35, 0.7, 0.5)` against `(0.5, 0.7, 0.3)`. A reader who cited the initializer for the loan rate would have been wrong by 2× at both ends.
So where this reference sends a reader into `EconomyPrefab.cs`, `ExtractorParameterPrefab.cs`, `AttractivenessParametersPrefab.cs` or `OutsideTradeParameterPrefab.cs`, it states once, as a trap, that the literals there are Unity-serialized defaults the shipped asset overrides, with nothing in the C# marking which survived.

## Findings

### The vocabulary, in one pass

Five words carry this topic and the code separates them precisely.

- **Resource** — a member of `Game.Economy.Resource`, a `ulong` enum whose members are powers of two and which carries **no `[Flags]` attribute** (`src/Game/Game.Economy/Resource.cs:3-49`). 41 real resources plus `NoResource = 0`, `Last` and `All`. It is a bitmask in use regardless, so a *set* of resources is one value and every test is `(a & b) != 0` — `EconomyUtils.CountResources` counts the set bits by iterating (`EconomyUtils.cs:222-234`) and `ResourceIterator` is the loop everything uses to walk it, shifting left from 1 and stopping at `Last` (`src/Game/Game.Economy/ResourceIterator.cs:9-21`).
  Two conversions are needed constantly and are easy to confuse: **`EconomyUtils.GetResourceIndex(resource)` is a 41-arm switch from bit to dense index** (`EconomyUtils.cs:26-143`, with `ResourceCount = GetResourceIndex(Resource.Last) = 41` at `:24`), and `GetResource(int index)` is `1L << index` (`:213-220`). Every per-resource array in this topic is indexed by the dense index, every component field holds the bit, and a serialized single-resource field writes the index as an `sbyte` while a serialized resource-set field writes the raw `ulong` mask. `ResourceInEditor` is a third representation — a plain sequential enum for authoring, converted by `GetResource(ResourceInEditor)` as `index - 1` (`:208-211`, the enum at `src/Game/Game.Economy/ResourceInEditor.cs:6-51`).
- **Resource prefab** — a `ResourcePrefab : PrefabBase` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/ResourcePrefab.cs:9`) carrying a `ResourceData` component (`ResourceData.cs:6-41`). One per resource; 41 live.
- **Industrial process** — an `IndustrialProcessData { m_Input1, m_Input2, m_Output, m_WorkPerUnit, m_MaxWorkersPerCell, m_IsImport }` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/IndustrialProcessData.cs:6-19`) on a **company prefab**. Each `m_Input*`/`m_Output` is a `ResourceStack { m_Resource, m_Amount }` (`ResourceStack.cs:6-10`). **This is the recipe, and it is the production graph.**
- **Company** — an entity instantiated from a `CompanyPrefab : ArchetypePrefab` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/CompanyPrefab.cs:15`). It has a `zone` (`AreaType.Commercial` or `AreaType.Industrial`) and gains `CompanyData`, `UpdateFrame`, `Resources`, `PropertySeeker`, `TripNeeded`, `CompanyNotifications`, `GuestVehicle` plus `CommercialCompany` or `IndustrialCompany` (`:36-54`).
- **Company role** is a set of empty tag components rather than a field: `CommercialCompany`, `IndustrialCompany`, `OfficeCompany`, `ProcessingCompany`, `ExtractorCompany`, `TransportCompany` (all `Size = 1`, `IEmptySerializable`, in `src/Game/Game.Companies/`), plus `StorageCompany` and `BuyingCompany` which carry a field each. **Presence of the tag is the state**, exactly as `citizens-and-households` records for citizens.

Which tags a company gets is decided by which `ComponentBase` the prefab author attached:

- `Game.Prefabs.ProcessingCompany` (`ProcessingCompany.cs:13`) adds prefab-side `IndustrialProcessData` and archetype-side `ProcessingCompany`, `TaxPayer`, `ResourceSeller`, `Profitability`, `TradeCost`, `CompanyStatisticData`; **`BuyingCompany` only if the process has an input** (`:32-35`); **`LodgingProvider` + `Renter` only if the output is `Lodging`** (`:36-40`); **`OfficeCompany` only if `EconomyUtils.IsOfficeResource(output)`** (`:41-44`); `TransportCompany` + `OwnedVehicle` only if `transports > 0` (`:49-53`).
- `Game.Prefabs.ServiceCompany` (`ServiceCompany.cs:10`) adds prefab-side `ServiceCompanyData` and archetype-side `ServiceAvailable`. **`ServiceAvailable`'s presence is what the whole codebase uses to mean "this is a commercial company"** — `isIndustrial` is spelled `!chunk.Has(ref m_ServiceAvailableType)` (`ProcessingCompanySystem.cs:136`, `CompanyProfitabilitySystem.cs:76`, `CompanyEconomyStatisticSystem.cs:135`).
- `Game.Prefabs.StorageCompany` (`StorageCompany.cs:13`) adds `StorageCompanyData` + `IndustrialProcessData` prefab-side and `StorageCompany`, `TradeCost`, `ResourceSeller`, `StorageTransferRequest` archetype-side. It notably does **not** add `ProcessingCompany`, which is how warehouses fall out of every processing system's query.
- `Game.Prefabs.ExtractorCompany` (`ExtractorCompany.cs:9`) adds `ExtractorCompanyData` and the `ExtractorCompany` tag and nothing else.

**Verified live**: a commercial hotel company (entity 54049, prefab 377 `Commercial_Hotel`) carried 28 components — the archetype above plus `Target`, `PropertyRenter`, `FreeWorkplaces`, `Renter`, `LodgingProvider`, `ServiceAvailable` and the `CommercialCompany`/`ProcessingCompany`/`TransportCompany`/`ResourceSeller` tags, with `UpdateFrame` as its shared component. Read with `ecs_list_components`.

### The production graph, in full, from the game's own declaration of it

**The graph lives in `IndustrialProcessData` on company prefabs, and nowhere else.** There is no table, no chart asset and no chain system: each recipe is one component on one company prefab, and the "graph" is the join over all of them by resource.

**Ruled (2026-08-10, the economy-and-companies pass under the maintainer's delegated authority; conflicts.md).** The graph bakes at **resource level**, in full, and no prefab-name column ships. A mod reaches a recipe by query — `IndustrialProcessData` filtered on its output resource, or the `ProcessEstimate` buffer as the game's own resource-to-process join, through which no retail, converter or warehouse recipe resolves — and never by name, so the names are not part of the mechanism; they are a swept roster of one install's DLC-extensible asset set, which ADR 0006 ships as shape or derivation and never as the list. What the reference owes because of it: every edge, grouped by company family (extractor, industrial, office, commercial converter, retail, warehouse), amounts absent as already ruled; the query and `ProcessEstimate` as the access shape beside the table; the warehouse and retail rows as their **rules** — one `Industrial_Warehouse<Resource>` per material and material good, none for the four office resources; retail as the `X -> X` pass-through `ProcessingCompanySystem` short-circuits on — never as prefab rosters; the naming conventions as shape statements; a prefab name only as a cited worked example, at most one per family; and the derivation stated — the edge set was enumerated from one install's prefab set, and the query that produced it is the check. The prefab-name tables below stay in this file as the record; the amounts are settled separately and do not ship in any form (see the mode-invariance paragraph at the end of this finding).

**Method for the table below.** `ecs_query` on `Game.Prefabs.IndustrialProcessData`, labelled through `Game.Prefabs.PrefabSystem:GetPrefabName`, returned **83** prefabs in the user's install; the **55** non-warehouse components were each read with `eval` and are all of the recipe table. Both are `zoning-buildings-and-land-value`'s scoring functions, and that file records the score expressions without recording that this adjacency table feeds them.
`Rots:` the mismatches — re-derive both sides rather than trusting either, and re-check whether the callers are still these two.

**The edges are mode-invariant; the amounts are not.** `Game.Prefabs.Modes.ProcessingCompanyGlobalMode` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs.Modes/ProcessingCompanyGlobalMode.cs:24-35`) is a game-mode pass whose whole job is `m_Input1.m_Amount *= m_InputMultiplier; m_Input2.m_Amount *= m_InputMultiplier; m_Output.m_Amount *= m_OutputMultiplier` over **every** entity with `IndustrialProcessData` (`:50-55`). It never touches `m_Resource`. So a game mode can halve every recipe's yield and cannot re-wire the graph — which is exactly the mechanism/balance line the reference needs: the edges ship, the amounts do not. That mode is not in `EasyMode`'s 21, so the amounts read live are the asset's.

### What a resource is, and the flags that decide which economy it belongs to

`ResourceData` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/ResourceData.cs:6-41`) is derived from the `ResourcePrefab` ScriptableObject at load: `Game.Prefabs.ResourceSystem` copies seventeen fields across for every prefab carrying `Created` + `PrefabData` + `ResourceData` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/ResourceSystem.cs:111-128`) and interns the entity in a resource-indexed array (`:131-133`), which is what `ResourcePrefabs[resource]` then indexes (`ResourcePrefabs.cs:11-22`). It also accumulates `BaseConsumptionSum` from `HouseholdBehaviorSystem.GetWeight` at wealth 200 (`:129`, `:145`).

Five booleans and one float do all the categorising, and every one of them is a prefab value that ships as a field:

- **`m_Weight == 0` means immaterial.** This single test, not any enum, is what the code calls "office": `ProcessingCompanySystem.IsOffice(process)` is `!EconomyUtils.IsResourceHasWeight(process.m_Output.m_Resource, ...)` (`ProcessingCompanySystem.cs:321-324`), and `TaxSystem.PayTaxJob` reclassifies an industrial taxpayer as `IncomeSource.TaxOffice` when the output resource's weight is zero (`TaxSystem.cs:108-112`). Weightless output also swaps the storage cap from `StorageLimitData.m_Limit` to `IndustrialAISystem.kMaxVirtualResourceStorage` (`ProcessingCompanySystem.cs:221-229`) and skips the pathfind on a purchase.
- **`m_IsMaterial`** marks an extracted raw material and is what `IndustrialSpawnSystem` branches on to decide whether a missing producer means "spawn an extractor" or "spawn a factory" (`IndustrialSpawnSystem.cs:134-172`).
- **`m_IsProduceable`** gates a resource out of company spawning entirely (`IndustrialSpawnSystem.cs:133`).
- **`m_IsTradable`** gates a resource out of the outside-connection trade balance and out of warehouse spawning (`BudgetSystem.cs:242`, `IndustrialSpawnSystem.cs:174-177`).
- **`m_IsLeisure`** gates a resource out of baseline household consumption unless leisure is asked for — `HouseholdBehaviorSystem`'s weight functions read it as `(leisureIncluded || !m_IsLeisure) ? m_BaseConsumption : 0` (`HouseholdBehaviorSystem.cs:615/617/630`); which resources carry it is authored per prefab, and the live read below found it on the four commercial services. (Corrected 2026-08-10, the mechanical pass, from "marks the four commercial services" — the flag is a consumption gate, and the membership is asset data, not C#.)
- **`m_RequireNaturalResource`** is the second half of the extractor gate; see resource extraction below.

**Live flag readings (2026-08-10, `ecs_get_component` per resource prefab), recorded because the *pattern* is the finding and the reference states the fields rather than the table.** All ten materials have `m_IsMaterial = true`, `m_IsProduceable = true`, `m_IsTradable = true`, `m_IsLeisure = false` and nonzero weight. `m_RequireNaturalResource` is **true for eight and false for Livestock and Stone** — which is the wiki's "livestock and stone mining need no natural resource" claim, verified first-party and stated as a field rather than as a list. Cotton alone carries `m_RequiredTemperature = 10` with `m_RequireTemperature = false`, so the temperature gate is authored and disabled.
All eighteen material goods have `m_IsMaterial = false`, `m_IsProduceable = true`, `m_IsTradable = true`, and weights from 0.5 (Pharmaceuticals) to 10 (Concrete). The four office resources have `m_Weight = 0` and `m_IsTradable = true`. The four leisure resources — Lodging, Meals, Entertainment, Recreation — have `m_IsProduceable = false`, `m_IsTradable = false` and `m_IsLeisure = true`; **Meals has weight 1 while the other three have 0**, so Meals is the one leisure resource the `m_Weight == 0` "office" test does not catch. `Money` and `Garbage` are neither produceable nor tradable.
Balance recorded as evidence only: `m_Price` is a `float2` whose `.x` is the industrial price and `.y` the commercial margin (`EconomyUtils.GetIndustrialPrice`/`GetServicePrice`/`GetMarketPrice`, `EconomyUtils.cs:1215-1258`), and every material reads `(0.5, 0)` while goods run from `(10, 25)` for Petrochemicals to `(65, 65)` for Financial and Media. `m_NeededWorkPerUnit` is an `int2` of (industrial work, commercial work): `(2, 0)` for every material, `(25..70, 10..25)` for everything else.

### The production formula, and the four things that multiply into it

`EconomyUtils.GetCompanyProductionPerDay` (`src/Game/Game.Economy/EconomyUtils.cs:1477-1493`) is the whole of production and it is small:

```
sectorEfficiency = IsExtractorResource(output) ? m_ExtractorProductionEfficiency
                 : isIndustrial              ? m_IndustrialEfficiency
                 :                             m_CommercialEfficiency
work    = buildingEfficiency * sectorEfficiency * GetWorkforce(employees, citizens) * kCompanyUpdatesPerDay
perUnit = isIndustrial ? resourceData.m_NeededWorkPerUnit.x : .y
units   = ceil(output.m_Amount * work / perUnit)
```

and then, **for a commercial company only**, a taper on its own stock of service:

```
saturation = serviceAvailable.m_ServiceAvailable / serviceCompanyData.m_MaxService
if (saturation >= 0.8)  units = ceil(lerp(units, 0, saturate((saturation - 0.8) / 0.2)))
```

`EconomyUtils.kCompanyUpdatesPerDay = 256` is a `static readonly int` (`:22`) and ships as a number; so do the `0.8f` and `0.2f` taper constants. **A commercial company that is 100% stocked produces nothing** — that is the mechanism behind a shop idling, and it is a hard zero rather than a slowdown.

`GetWorkforce` is the citizens' contribution: `sum over the Employee buffer of GetWorkerWorkforce(citizen.Happiness, employee.m_Level)` (`:1402-1414`), where `GetWorkerWorkforce(happiness, level) = ((level == 0 ? 2 : 1) + 2.5 * level) * (0.75 + happiness/200)` (`:1447-1450`). `citizens-and-households` owns that function and its ladder.

**Employee happiness enters production twice, and the second entry is easy to miss.** `GetWorkforce` above reads each employee's happiness directly. Separately, `WorkProviderSystem` writes `EfficiencyFactor.EmployeeHappiness` into the *building's* `Efficiency` buffer as `currentWorkforce / averageWorkforce + workConditions * 0.01`, where `currentWorkforce` sums `GetWorkerWorkforce(happiness, level)` and `averageWorkforce` sums `GetWorkerWorkforce(50, level)` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/WorkProviderSystem.cs:260-281`, the helper at `:314-333`) — and `buildingEfficiency` is the product of every factor in that buffer. So a happiness change moves the same term twice, multiplicatively. `WorkplaceData.m_WorkConditions` is documented on the authoring component as "Offset to employee happiness" (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/Workplace.cs:28-29`) and enters only through that second path.

**Building efficiency is a product of an enumerated factor list, and one factor is excluded when production reads it.** `Efficiency { m_Factor, m_Efficiency }` is an `[InternalBufferCapacity(8)]` buffer on the building (`src/Game/Game.Buildings/Efficiency.cs:7-12`); `BuildingUtils.GetEfficiency` multiplies every entry, clamps each at ≥ 0, and returns `max(0.01, round(100 * product) / 100)` — so efficiency is quantised to whole percent and floored at 1% unless a factor is at or below 0 (the guard is `!(product > 0)`), in which case it is 0 (`src/Game/Game.Buildings/BuildingUtils.cs:121-133`). `EfficiencyFactor` is a 32-member enum plus a `Count` sentinel (`src/Game/Game.Buildings/EfficiencyFactor.cs:3-37`) — against an `[InternalBufferCapacity(8)]` buffer, so a building with more than eight active factors spills to the heap — and **that enum is the answer to "what are a company's profitability inputs"**: `Destroyed, Abandoned, Disabled, Fire, ServiceBudget, NotEnoughEmployees, SickEmployees, EmployeeHappiness, ElectricitySupply, ElectricityFee, WaterSupply, DirtyWater, SewageHandling, WaterFee, Garbage, Telecom, Mail, MaterialSupply, WindSpeed, WaterDepth, SunIntensity, NaturalResources, CityModifierSoftware, CityModifierElectronics, CityModifierIndustrialEfficiency, CityModifierOfficeEfficiency, CityModifierHospitalEfficiency, SpecializationBonus, CityModifierFishInput, CityModifierFishHub, LackResources, GateBypass`.
`ProcessingCompanySystem` reads `GetEfficiencyExcludingFactor(buffer, EfficiencyFactor.LackResources)` rather than `GetEfficiency` (`ProcessingCompanySystem.cs:166`), because it is about to *write* `LackResources` itself from whether the inputs allowed any output at all (`:196-199`). Reading the plain efficiency there would feed last tick's shortage back into this tick's production.
Which factors this topic owns the writers of: `SpecializationBonus`, `CityModifierIndustrialEfficiency`, `CityModifierOfficeEfficiency`, `CityModifierSoftware`, `CityModifierElectronics` and `CityModifierFishInput` are written by `ProcessingCompanySystem.UpdateEfficiencyFactors` (`:278-319`; the Software/Electronics pair is an if/else, one per company; corrected 2026-08-10, review gate: the Office/Industrial pair is not — an office output takes the office factor, pinned at 1 with the modifier skipped when the company is commercial, only a non-commercial non-office output takes the industrial factor, and an ordinary commercial company gets neither), while `LackResources` is written by the same system's chunk loop after the input caps (`:198`) — corrected 2026-08-10, the mechanical pass, from an original that put it in `UpdateEfficiencyFactors`; `NaturalResources` and `CityModifierFishHub` by `ExtractorCompanySystem` (`:218-225`); `NotEnoughEmployees`, `SickEmployees`, `EmployeeHappiness` by `WorkProviderSystem` (`:279-281`); the rest belong to `city-services-and-coverage` and `utilities-and-flow-networks`.

**The city specialization bonus is asymptotic, and the wiki's headline number describes it as a threshold.**
`ProductionSpecializationSystem` (`SystemOrder.cs:452`, `kUpdatesPerDay = 512`, interval `262144 / 512`, `src/Game/Game.Simulation/ProductionSpecializationSystem.cs:74`, `:86-89`) accumulates every produced unit into a resource-indexed `SpecializationBonus` buffer on the city entity and then decays the whole buffer by `floor(0.999 * value)` on every pass (`:36-59`). `SpecializationBonus.GetBonus(maxBonus, coefficient) = maxBonus * value / (value + coefficient)` (`src/Game/Game.City/SpecializationBonus.cs:15-18`), and `ProcessingCompanySystem` writes `1 + GetBonus(m_MaxCitySpecializationBonus, m_ResourceProductionCoefficient)` as `EfficiencyFactor.SpecializationBonus` (`:313-318`).
Verdict: **the wiki's "Default max value of 115% at 10kt of production" states an asymptote as a threshold, and neither the initialized nor the live coefficient reproduces the figure.** The expression `1 + 0.15·v/(v + C)` approaches 1.15 and never reaches it. `EconomyPrefab` initializes `m_MaxCitySpecializationBonus = 0.15f` and `m_ResourceProductionCoefficient = 10000000` (`EconomyPrefab.cs:92`, `:95`); the live singleton reads 0.15 and **1,000,000**. Live, the largest of the 39 `SpecializationBonus` entries in the user's city was 35,564, giving a bonus of 0.0051 — half a percent, not fifteen. First-party wins with no contest: the shape is the finding, the ceiling is a parameter, and the "at 10 kt" is not a fact about anything the code computes. What ships is the formula, the decay, and the two field names.

### `CompanyProfitabilitySystem` reads the process data off the wrong entity

`Profitability { byte m_Profitability, int m_LastTotalWorth }` (`src/Game/Game.Companies/Profitability.cs:6-10`) is the number the company-profitability infoview colours buildings by, and it is a **delta in total worth**, not an income statement:

```
m_Profitability = clamp((totalWorth - m_LastTotalWorth) / 100, -127, 128) + 127
m_LastTotalWorth = totalWorth
```

(`src/Game/Game.Simulation/CompanyProfitabilitySystem.cs:92-93`, `kUpdatesPerDay = 1` at `:175`, interval `262144 / (1 * 16)` at `:187-190`.) **127 is break-even**, and the clamp saturates the byte at a worth change of −12,700 and +12,800 — asymmetrically, because the clamp bounds are −127 and 128. The same job also rolls `CompanyStatisticData`'s monthly customer and purchase counters over, moving `m_CurrentNumberOfCustomers` into `m_MonthlyCustomerCount` and tracking a high-water `m_MaxNumberOfCustomers` (`:101-113`) — so "monthly" in that component means "since the last daily profitability pass".

**The defect.** `EconomyUtils.GetCompanyTotalWorth` needs the company's `IndustrialProcessData` to know which resource is the company's *output*, because a commercial company's output stock is valued at the market price (industrial + margin) while everything else is valued at the industrial price (`EconomyUtils.cs:511-524`). `CompanyProfitabilityJob` fetches it like this:

```
if (m_PrefabRefs.TryGetComponent(entity, out var _))
{
    m_IndustrialProcessDatas.TryGetComponent(entity, out componentData);
}
```

(`CompanyProfitabilitySystem.cs:83-86`.) It resolves the `PrefabRef` and **discards it**, then looks `IndustrialProcessData` up on the **company entity**, which never carries it: `ProcessingCompany.GetPrefabComponents` and `StorageCompany.GetPrefabComponents` add it prefab-side only (`ProcessingCompany.cs:21`, `StorageCompany.cs:22`), and every other reader in the game goes through the prefab — `BuildingUpkeepSystem.cs:209`, `ProcessingCompanySystem.cs:159`, `CompanyMoveAwaySystem.cs:113`, `CompanyEconomyStatisticSystem.cs:172`, `CountCompanyDataSystem.cs:344`, `CommercialAISystem.cs:105`, `ServiceCompanySystem.cs:135` all pass `prefab`. A sweep of `src/Game/` for a producer of `IndustrialProcessData` on an instance archetype returns none.
So `componentData` stays `default`, its output resource is `NoResource`, and a **commercial** company's whole inventory is valued at the industrial price rather than the market price — undervaluing exactly the stock the company exists to sell. `Rots:` this reading; it is the kind of line a patch fixes silently. `Unconfirmed:` the size of the resulting error, which would need the same company's worth computed both ways in a running game; the live read that would settle it is `EconomyUtils.GetCompanyTotalWorth` called twice through `eval` with a real and a defaulted process, and this pass did not run it.
Recorded because a mod that reads `Profitability` inherits the bug, and a mod that fixes it changes the infoview.

**Live evidence, with the pause caveat.** A grain-extractor company (entity 257243, prefab 374) read `m_Profitability = 105`, `m_LastTotalWorth = -4294`. The hotel at 54049 read `m_Profitability = 127`, `m_LastTotalWorth = 0`, with a `Resources` buffer of Food 6483, **Money −467,152** and Lodging 5086. `CompanyStatisticData`'s every money field was 0 on both, which the paused simulation is the likeliest explanation for and which no static read contradicts.

**A second profitability function exists and reads a different field.** `CompanyUtils.GetCompanyProfitability(profit, economyParameterData)` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/CompanyUtils.cs:50-75`) maps `CompanyStatisticData.m_Profit` onto 0–255 by lerping across `m_ProfitabilityRange.x → 127` for a loss and `127 → m_ProfitabilityRange.y` for a profit, returning 127 when the range is degenerate. Its callers are `ObjectColorSystem.cs:1005` and three sites in the company infoview's UI system (`src/Game/Game.UI.InGame/CompanyInfoviewUISystem.cs:103/136/174`). So **the building colour and the `Profitability` component are two different numbers with the same name and the same 127 midpoint**, and only one of them is the worth delta. That is worth a trap.

### Where a company's money actually comes from and goes

A company holds no money field: money is `Resource.Money` in its `Game.Economy.Resources` buffer (`src/Game/Game.Economy/Resources.cs:6-10`), written through `EconomyUtils.AddResources` (`EconomyUtils.cs:320-338`), which accumulates and clamps to `int`, and `EconomyUtils.SetResources` (`:288`), which assigns — the divorce split and the city-service money sweep use the second (`DivorceSystem.cs:91/93`, `ResourceProducerSystem.cs:126-129`). (Corrected 2026-08-10, the mechanical pass, from "moved only through `AddResources`", and the grep claim beside it: a grep for `AddResources(Resource.Money` is a first sweep, not a census — a resource-agnostic loop over a whole buffer moves the money row without naming it, `PartnerSystem.cs:62`, and it misses `SetResources` sites and wages, `PayWageSystem.cs:120-127/154`.) The company-facing half is:

**Exporting a weighted resource to an outside connection mints money into the city's economy.** `ResourceExporterSystem.HandleExportsJob` computes the sale value but only credits it in the weightless branch (`ResourceExporterSystem.cs:262`, `:296`); a weighted export instead becomes a `Purpose.Exporting` `TripNeeded` and a `CurrentTrading` entry (`:131-156`), and the money arrives on delivery at `DeliveryTruckAISystem.cs:397`. In that branch the *target* is debited at `:401` unless it is a `StorageCompany` — and every outside connection **is** a `StorageCompany` (`TradeSystem`'s query is `StorageCompany` + `Game.Objects.OutsideConnection`, `TradeSystem.cs:329`). So an export credits the seller and debits nobody. Symmetrically an import debits the buyer and credits nobody. The outside world is a money source and sink by construction, and that is the mechanism behind an export-led city getting rich.

**A sale's price is not the resource's price.** `ResourceBuyerSystem.BuyJob` (`:164-266`) starts from `GetMarketPrice` for a commercial seller and `GetIndustrialPrice` otherwise (`:171`), adds the seller's `TradeCost.m_BuyCost` per unit (`:176`), and then — for a commercial seller with a property — multiplies by `EconomyUtils.GetServicePriceMultiplier(m_ServiceAvailable, m_MaxService)` = `lerp(0.7, 1.3, saturate(1 - available/max))` (`EconomyUtils.cs:548-551`, applied at `:212`). **A shop with empty shelves charges 1.3× and a full one 0.7×**, which is the opposite of what a reader expects and is the only demand-side price signal in the game. The same call decrements `ServiceAvailable` and lerps `m_MeanPriority` toward the new saturation at alpha 0.1 (`:213-221`).

**Dividends are a fixed fraction, once a day.** `CompanyDividendSystem` (`SystemOrder.cs:499`, `kUpdatesPerDay = 1`, `src/Game/Game.Simulation/CompanyDividendSystem.cs:201`, `:215-218`): a company with a positive money balance pays each employee's household `max(0, money / (8 * employeeCount))` and debits itself `that * employeeCount` — so **one eighth of a company's cash goes to its workers' households every day**, split equally regardless of job level (`:116-139`). The `8` is a bare literal in the job.

**Bankruptcy is measured on total worth, not on money, and it needs to persist.** `CompanyMoveAwaySystem` (`SystemOrder.cs:505`, `kUpdatesPerDay = 16`) computes `GetCompanyTotalWorth` — money plus every stocked resource at price plus every loaded owned truck's cargo (`EconomyUtils.cs:469-524`) — and compares it against `EconomyParameterData.m_CompanyBankruptcyLimit`. Below the limit it stamps `CompanyStatisticData.m_LastFrameLowIncome` and only adds `MovingAway` once `|frame - m_LastFrameLowIncome| > 65536`, i.e. a quarter of a game day of continuous insolvency (`CompanyMoveAwaySystem.cs:132-144`). Above the limit the field is reset every pass (`:145-150`), so the timer needs an unbroken run.
**A separate and much faster exit exists: taxes and staffing complaints.** `CompanyUtils.GetCompanyMoveAwayChance` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/CompanyUtils.cs:15-33`) returns `(taxRate - 10) * 5 / 2`, **+5 if the company currently has an uneducated-workers notification and +20 if it has an educated-workers notification**, and `CompanyMoveAwaySystem` rolls `random.NextInt(100) < chance` every pass (`:127-131`). The tax rate used is the per-resource commercial, industrial or office rate depending on whether the company has `ServiceAvailable` and whether its property has `OfficeProperty`. So at the 30% cap the tax term alone is a 50% chance per pass, 16 passes a day. The `10` neutral rate, the `5/2`, the `5` and the `20` are bare literals and ship as part of the formula.

**`CompanyEconomyStatisticSystem` is the income statement, and it is a *projection* rather than a ledger.** `SystemOrder.cs:501`, `kUpdatesPerDay = 128` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/CompanyEconomyStatisticSystem.cs:344`, interval `262144 / (128*16)` at `:360-363`). Every pass it zeroes eleven fields and recomputes them from current state (`:124-134`): `m_RentPaid` from `PropertyRenter.m_Rent` plus a share of the building's `ConsumptionData.m_Upkeep` (`:140-153`), the utility fields from the property's consumer components times the city's current `ServiceFee` (`:156-167`), `m_WagePaid` from `CalculateTotalWage` over the `Employee` buffer (`:171`), `m_Income` and `m_CostBuyResource` from `GetCompanyProductionPerDay` at the *current* efficiency times price (`:188-213`), `m_GarbagePaid` from `ConsumptionData.m_GarbageAccumulation` times the garbage fee (`:218-223`), `m_Worth` from `GetCompanyTotalWorth` (`:224-231`), and finally `m_Profit = m_Income - (everything else)` (`:232-234`). None of it is what the company actually paid; it is what it would pay at this instant's rates. `IndustrialAISystem` then hires and fires off `m_LastUpdateWorth` and `m_Profit` from this system (`IndustrialAISystem.cs:137-139`), so the projection drives real behaviour.
Two asymmetries in it worth a trap: the commercial branch's `m_TaxPaid` is `ceil(production * servicePrice) * m_AverageTaxRate / 100` while the industrial branch's is `(m_Income - m_CostBuyResource) * m_AverageTaxRate / 100` (`:198` against `:213`) — different bases; and the `m_RentPaid` upkeep share is added only `if (upkeep < value.m_Worth)` (`:149`), reading `m_Worth` while it is still 0 from the zeroing at `:125`, so **the upkeep share is never added**. `Rots:` both.

### How a company hires, how many jobs it has, and what that has to do with output

`WorkProvider { m_MaxWorkers, m_UneducatedCooldown, m_EducatedCooldown, m_UneducatedNotificationEntity, m_EducatedNotificationEntity, m_EfficiencyCooldown }` (`src/Game/Game.Companies/WorkProvider.cs:6-18`) holds the headcount target; the actual staff is the `Employee { m_Worker, m_Level }` buffer (`Employee.cs:6-10`), and `FreeWorkplaces` is a five-byte cache of vacancies by level with `GetBestFor(level)` walking **down** from the seeker's education (`FreeWorkplaces.cs:49-59`). `citizens-and-households` owns the hiring side of that.

**`m_MaxWorkers` is a target the AI walks one step at a time, and the step rules differ per sector.** All three AI systems run at `kUpdatesPerDay = 32` with `kMinimumEmployee = 5`:

- **Commercial** (`CommercialAISystem.cs:115-123`, constants at `:223-227`): decrement if `m_MaxWorkers > 5` and the company is at or above `m_MaxService`; increment if fully staffed, more than one fitting worker spare, and service at or below `m_MaxService / 4`. So a commercial company sheds staff when its shelves are full and hires when they are a quarter full.
- **Industrial** (`IndustrialAISystem.cs:137-176`, constants at `:284-294`): four ordered tests. Worth below `kMinWorthRequire = -50000` → decrement by 2 if also unprofitable, else by 1. Worth below `kMinWorthRequirePositiveProfit = -10000` and unprofitable → decrement. Stock at or above half the storage limit **and** city demand below city production for the output resource → decrement. Fully staffed with room and stock at or below a quarter of the limit → increment. Then `clamp(m_MaxWorkers, 5, fittingWorkers)`. A weightless (office) output substitutes `kMaxVirtualResourceStorage = 100000` and its half for the storage limit and its quarter (`:149-150`).
- **Extractor** (`ExtractorAISystem.cs:104-118`, constants at `:201-205`): decrement if `m_MaxWorkers > kMinimumEmployee` and stock is at or above the (upgrade-combined) storage limit — that guard is the only floor, there being no closing clamp; otherwise, when fully staffed with room, **jump straight to `min(kMaximumInitEmployee, fittingWorkers)` if below `kMaximumInitEmployee = 80`**, else increment by one. So an extractor staffs up to 80 in one step and crawls after that, where the other two sectors only ever move by one.

**Fitting workers is a per-cell density on the recipe, not on the workplace.** `CompanyUtils` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/CompanyUtils.cs:35-48`): commercial is `ceil(ServiceCompanyData.m_MaxWorkersPerCell * lotX * lotY * (1 + 0.5 * level) * m_SpaceMultiplier)`; industrial and office is the same with `IndustrialProcessData.m_MaxWorkersPerCell`; an extractor is `max(1, ceil(m_MaxWorkersPerCell * area / 2))` where `area` sums, over the hub's and its installed upgrades' sub-areas that carry a `Lot`, `Geometry.m_SurfaceArea / 64` (`ExtractorAISystem.cs:265-275`), and the formula's `spaceMultiplier` parameter is passed `1` at every call site (corrected 2026-08-10, the mechanical pass, from a version missing the `/64`, the `Lot` filter and the upgrades). The `1 + 0.5 * level`, the `/ 2` and the `/ 64` are literals and ship. `GetCompanyMaxFittingWorkers` (`:77-104`) is the dispatcher and its order matters: `ServiceCompanyData` first, then `ExtractorCompanyData`, then `IndustrialProcessData`.

**How `m_MaxWorkers` becomes jobs by level** is `EconomyUtils.CalculateNumberOfWorkplaces(totalWorkers, complexity, buildingLevel)` (`EconomyUtils.cs:1370-1400`), a triangular distribution centred at `4 * (int)complexity + buildingLevel - 1` with per-level weight `max(0, 8 - |centre - 4*level|)` out of 16 and the tails folded into levels 0 and 4. `citizens-and-households` already records it; what this topic adds is that **`WorkplaceComplexity` comes off the company prefab's `WorkplaceData`, and the level off the rented building's `SpawnableBuildingData`** — a company has no level of its own.

**A company's total wage bill** is `EconomyUtils.CalculateTotalWage` over the `Employee` buffer (`:1349-1357`), or over a hypothetical staffing via `CalculateNumberOfWorkplaces` (`:1359-1368`). `GetCompanyMaxProfitPerDay` (`:1452-1457`) is the fully-staffed, full-efficiency projection `profitPerUnit * production(maxWorkers) - totalWage(maxWorkers)`, and `zoning-buildings-and-land-value` records that `RentAdjustSystem` compares the asked rent against it.

**Profit per unit is a one-line function and the only place the recipe's economics live**: `GetCompanyProfitPerUnit` (`:1459-1462`) is
`((isIndustrial ? industrialPrice(output) : marketPrice(output)) * output.m_Amount - input1.m_Amount * industrialPrice(input1) - input2.m_Amount * industrialPrice(input2)) / output.m_Amount`.
A commercial company sells at the market price and buys at the industrial price; an industrial one does both at the industrial price. That asymmetry — `ResourceData.m_Price.y` accruing only to commercial sellers — is the retail margin, and it is why a pass-through retail company can be profitable at all.

### The city's money: taxation

**A tax rate is not a component.** `TaxSystem` owns a persistent `NativeArray<int>` of 92 entries (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/TaxSystem.cs:345`) handed out by `GetTaxRates()` (`:363-366`) with an `AddReader(JobHandle)` protocol (`:368-371`, `Readers` at `:235`). **The layout is documented by an enum in a different namespace**: `Game.City.TaxRate { Main = 0, ResidentialOffset = 1, CommercialOffset = 2, IndustrialOffset = 3, OfficeOffset = 4, EducationZeroOffset = 5, CommercialResourceZeroOffset = 10, IndustrialResourceZeroOffset = 51, Count = 92 }` (`src/Game/Game.City/TaxRate.cs:3-14`). Slots 1–4 are indexed by `TaxAreaType { None, Residential, Commercial, Industrial, Office }` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/TaxAreaType.cs:3-10`); 5–9 by job level; 10–50 and 51–91 by `EconomyUtils.GetResourceIndex`. **Industrial and office share the 51–91 block**, which is why `SetIndustrialTaxRate` and `SetOfficeTaxRate` write the same slot (`:728-733`, `:745-750`).

Every rate is a **sum of a global and an offset**, never stored absolutely:

```
GetTaxRate(area)              = rates[0] + rates[(int)area]
GetResidentialTaxRate(level)  = GetTaxRate(Residential) + rates[5 + level]
GetCommercialTaxRate(res)     = GetTaxRate(Commercial)  + rates[10 + resourceIndex]
GetIndustrialTaxRate(res)     = GetTaxRate(Industrial)  + rates[51 + resourceIndex]
GetOfficeTaxRate(res)         = GetTaxRate(Office)      + rates[51 + resourceIndex]
```

(`:444-447`, `:679-682`, `:696-699`, `:723-726`, `:740-743`.) So **a mod that writes a rate must write an offset, and setting the global rate re-clamps every offset**: the `TaxRate` property setter calls `EnsureAreaTaxRateLimits` for all four areas (`:219-233`), which in turn re-limits the job levels and the resources its own per-area tests pick — `IsCommercialResource`, produceable-with-weight, `IsOfficeResource`, not the taxable bit — against `TaxParameterData`'s seven `int2` limits (`:488-545`; corrected 2026-08-10, review gate, from "every taxable resource"). `SetDefaults` writes 10 into slot 0 and 0 everywhere else (`:317-324`), and `PostDeserialize` restores that for a save older than `Version.averageTaxRate` (`:351-361`). **Live: the whole array read `main = 10` and every offset 0**, so all four effective rates were 10.
`TaxParameterData` is a bare seven-`int2` singleton (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/TaxParameterData.cs:7-21`) written by `TaxParameterPrefab.LateInitialize` (`:36-50`). Live, all seven limits read `(-10, 30)`.
Verdict: **the wiki's "each tax can be set to be between -10% and 30%" is correct at 1.6.0f1 and is a prefab value.** The range is `TaxParameterData`'s seven fields, not a constant, so the reference names the component and the fields and states no number.

**Tax is charged from an accrual on the payer, not computed at collection.** `TaxPayer { m_UntaxedIncome, m_AverageTaxRate, m_AverageTaxPaid }` (`src/Game/Game.Agents/TaxPayer.cs:6-12`) accumulates as income arrives; `TaxSystem.GetTax(payer) = round(0.01 * m_AverageTaxRate * m_UntaxedIncome)` (`:271-274`) and `PayTaxJob.PayTax` deducts `round(m_PaidMultiplier * tax)` from the payer's money, enqueues a `StatisticsEvent`, then **zeroes `m_UntaxedIncome` and writes `m_AverageTaxPaid = tax * kUpdatesPerDay`** (`:69-124`). `kUpdatesPerDay = 32` (`:191`), interval `262144 / (32 * 16)` (`:237-240`).
Three separate jobs run over three queries, one per `IncomeSource`, and the queries are the definition of who pays what (`:340-342`):

- **residential** — `TaxPayer` + `UpdateFrame` + `Resources` + `Household`;
- **commercial** — `TaxPayer` + `UpdateFrame` + `Resources` + **`ServiceAvailable`**;
- **industrial** — `TaxPayer` + `UpdateFrame` + `Resources` + `ProcessingCompany`, **excluding `StorageCompany` and `ServiceAvailable`**.

**Office has no query.** An office company is caught by the industrial one and reclassified inside `PayTax`: if the prefab's output resource has `m_Weight == 0`, `taxType` becomes `IncomeSource.TaxOffice` and the statistic becomes `OfficeTaxableIncome` (`:104-113`). (Corrected 2026-08-10, review gate: the `taxType` write is a dead store — nothing reads it after `:110`, only `statisticType` is enqueued — so the rebooking is statistics-only, and the budget's `TaxOffice` row fills from the statistic.) So office tax exists as a *rate* and as a *statistic* and never as a query — and `m_PaidMultiplier` is taken from the industrial slot `m_TaxPaidMultiplier.z` regardless (`:432`), because the multiplier is chosen per job rather than per company.
`m_TaxPaidMultiplier` is a `float3` latched in `OnGameLoaded` from `Game.Prefabs.Modes.ModeSettingData.m_TaxPaidMultiplier` when that singleton exists and is enabled, else `(1,1,1)` (`:242-259`). **So a game mode scales collected tax and nothing in the tax rates records it.**

`m_AverageTaxRate` is a running lerp toward the current rate weighted by how much income just arrived: `round(lerp(m_AverageTaxRate, currentRate, newIncome / (newIncome + m_UntaxedIncome)))` — written by `ProcessingCompanySystem.cs:257`, `ServiceCompanySystem.cs:164`, `ExtractorCompanySystem.cs:301` and, for households, `PayWageSystem`. A rate change therefore takes effect gradually per payer rather than at the next collection. (Corrected 2026-08-10, review gate: the three sites do not weight identically — `ServiceCompanySystem.cs:161` increments `m_UntaxedIncome` before its lerp at `:164`, so its weight is `a / (I + 2a)` against the others' `a / (I + a)`, half the step right after a collection and converging toward parity as income accrues.)

**Who accrues a company's untaxed income differs by company kind, and one kind accrues twice.**

- An **industrial or extractor** company accrues `GetCompanyProfitPerDay(...) / kCompanyUpdatesPerDay` in `ProcessingCompanySystem.cs:250-261` (extractors in `ExtractorCompanySystem.cs:295-304`), gated on `input.m_Resource != output.m_Resource && profit > 0`.
- A **commercial** company accrues `ceil(max(0, producedThisTick * GetServicePrice(output)))` in `ServiceCompanySystem.cs:158-167` — and, if its recipe is a *converter* rather than a pass-through, **also** the `ProcessingCompanySystem` term, because that system's query includes companies with `ServiceAvailable` and only the pass-through recipes hit the `continue` at `:174-177`. So `Commercial_Hotel`, `Commercial_Restaurant`, `Commercial_Bar` and `Commercial_RecreactionStore` accrue from two systems per tick while the twelve retail prefabs accrue from one. `Unconfirmed:` that this is unintended; nothing in the code marks it, and settling it means summing both accruals for one converter across a tick in a running game, which the paused session could not do.
- `ServiceCompanySystem` is also the only accrual site that applies a **district** modifier: it uses `GetModifiedCommercialTaxRate(resource, rates, district, modifiers)` when the property has a `CurrentDistrict` (`ServiceCompanySystem.cs:148-156`), which folds in `DistrictModifierType.LowCommercialTax` (`TaxSystem.cs:463-472`). No industrial or office accrual site does.

### The city's money: fees, service trade, budget and loans

**The player's money is one component and one system moves it.** `PlayerMoney` on the city entity carries a private `int m_Money` and a `bool m_Unlimited`; the `money` property returns `2000000000` outright when unlimited, and `const int kMaxMoney = 2000000000` clamps both ways (`src/Game/Game.City/PlayerMoney.cs:9-41`). That `const` ships.

**The income and expense enumerations are the answer to "where does the city's money come from and go", and they are two flat enums.**
`IncomeSource` — `TaxResidential, TaxCommercial, TaxIndustrial, FeeHealthcare, FeeElectricity, GovernmentSubsidy, FeeEducation, ExportElectricity, ExportWater, FeeParking, FeePublicTransport, TaxOffice, FeeGarbage, FeeWater, Count` (`src/Game/Game.City/IncomeSource.cs:3-19`).

**A negative tax is an expense, not a negative income.** `CityServiceBudgetSystem.UpdateDataJob` fills the four `Subsidy*` expense slots from `TaxSystem.GetEstimatedTaxAmount(area, TaxResultType.Expense, ...)` negated, and the four `Tax*` income slots from the same call with `TaxResultType.Income` (`CityServiceBudgetSystem.cs:157-168`, `:234-245`). `TaxResultType { Income = 1, Expense = -1, Any = 0 }` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/TaxResultType.cs:3-8`) and `MatchesResultType` (`TaxSystem.cs:823-834`) split the per-resource and per-job-level estimates by sign. value = 100 * lerp(interestRange.x, interestRange.y, saturate(loanAmount / max(1, creditworthiness)))
CityUtils.ApplyModifier(ref value, cityEffects, CityModifierType.LoanInterest)

### Resource extraction: what a deposit is worth, and what depletion does

**The extractor economy spans four namespaces, and the join is the hub building's `Attached.m_Parent`.** `Game.Areas.Extractor` is the per-area state, `Game.Prefabs`' `ExtractorAreaData` / `ExtractorFacilityData` / `ExtractorCompanyData` / `ExtractorParameterData` are the authored side, `Game.Companies.ExtractorCompany` is the company tag and `Game.Buildings.ExtractorFacility` the hub's own state.
`zoning-buildings-and-land-value` records the placement half — that specialized industry is a `Game.Areas` area with an `ExtractorArea` prefab component and a hub building carrying `ExtractorFacility`. What this topic owns is the production and depletion chain, and it is:

```
NaturalResourceCell.m_Ore/.m_Oil/.m_Fertility/.m_Fish  (the map grid, environment-and-pollution)
  -> Extractor.m_ResourceAmount, .m_MaxConcentration   (AreaResourceSystem, per area)
  -> GetEffectiveConcentration(params, feature, conc)   (ExtractorCompanySystem)
  -> EfficiencyFactor.NaturalResources on the hub's Efficiency buffer
  -> buildingEfficiency in GetCompanyProductionPerDay
  -> units produced -> profit -> TaxPayer accrual
```

The chain ends at the accrual: `CompanyMoveAwaySystem`'s query excludes `ExtractorCompany`, as does `IndustrialAISystem`'s, so an extractor company never goes bankrupt and never moves away.

`Game.Areas.Extractor { m_ResourceAmount, m_MaxConcentration, m_ExtractedAmount, m_WorkAmount, m_HarvestedAmount, m_TotalExtracted, m_WorkType }` (`src/Game/Game.Areas/Extractor.cs:7-21`) is the per-area state. `AreaResourceSystem` recomputes `m_ResourceAmount` and `m_MaxConcentration` from the grid cells the area's triangles cover, `m_MaxConcentration` as the area-weighted mean concentration clamped to 1 (`src/Game/Game.Areas/AreaResourceSystem.cs:387-388`, `:493-498`, `:562-568`).

**Concentration, not amount, is what sets efficiency, and the parameter is a *sufficiency* threshold rather than a stock.**
`ExtractorCompanySystem.GetEffectiveConcentration(params, feature, concentration) = min(1, concentration / X)` where X is `m_FullOil`, `m_FullFertility`, `m_FullFish` or `m_FullOre` by map feature and 1 for anything else (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/ExtractorCompanySystem.cs:707-717`). The authoring tooltip states the intent exactly: "If the resource concentration goes under this limit, the productivity starts to drop" (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/ExtractorParameterPrefab.cs:26-36`). `GetBestConcentration` averages the effective concentration over every sub-area and installed upgrade of the hub, weighted by `Geometry.m_SurfaceArea`, and **returns false when the result is zero, which skips the company's entire production pass** (`:660-705`, the early return at `:227-230`). The same value is written as `EfficiencyFactor.NaturalResources` before that check, so a depleted extractor's building shows 0% efficiency (`:224`).
**A resource that does not require a deposit bypasses all of it.** `GetBestConcentration`'s branch is `if (requireNaturalResource && componentData4.m_RequireNaturalResource)`, and otherwise it counts the whole surface area at concentration 1 (`:691-702`). Both flags must hold: `ResourceData.m_RequireNaturalResource` on the *resource* and `ExtractorAreaData.m_RequireNaturalResource` on the *area prefab*. `GetRequiredMapFeature` is the same conjunction (`:651-658`). Live, Livestock and Stone are the two resources with the flag off — so a livestock or stone area produces at full concentration wherever it is painted. (Corrected 2026-08-10, the mechanical pass: the original added "and never depletes anything" — the flags gate only the concentration multiplier and the per-area remaining cap; `m_ExtractedAmount` accrues outside the guard, `ExtractorCompanySystem.cs:436-437`, and the grid write-down keys on the area's map feature, `AreaLotSimulationSystem.cs:690-712`.)

**What extraction takes out of the ground, and why a deposit lasts longer as it empties.**
`ProcessArea` (`:413-441`) apportions the company's per-tick production over the hub's sub-areas by surface area, clamps a natural-resource area's share to `min(remaining, share * effectiveConcentration)` where `remaining = m_ResourceAmount - m_ExtractedAmount`, multiplies by a per-feature `GetExtractionMultiplier` — `m_FertilityConsumption`, `m_FishConsumption`, `m_ForestConsumption`, else 1 (`:443-457`) — and accumulates `m_ExtractedAmount`, `m_TotalExtracted` and `m_WorkAmount += share * ExtractorAreaData.m_WorkAmountFactor`.
The grid write-down is `AreaLotSimulationSystem`, which is where the two non-renewable parameters are spent. Fertile land and fish subtract the extracted amount from the cell directly; **ore and oil go through `GetUnlimitedUsage`** (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/AreaLotSimulationSystem.cs:720-724`):

```
mu = 1 / m_OreConsumption   (or 1 / m_OilConsumption)
n     = log(originalConcentration) - log(currentConcentration)
usage = RoundToIntRandom(mu * originalConcentration * exp(-n) * extractedAmount * 10000)
```

with the call sites at `:825` and `:833`, where `originalConcentration = m_Base * 0.0001` and `currentConcentration = (m_Base - m_Used) * 0.0001`.
**The expression collapses, and the collapsed form reproduces the authors' own tooltip exactly.** `exp(-(ln(o) - ln(c)))` is `c/o`, so `usage = mu * o * (c/o) * E * 10000 = mu * c * E * 10000`. Substituting `c = (base - used) * 0.0001` gives `d(used) = mu * (base - used) * dE`, i.e. the remaining concentration decays as `exp(-E / m_OreConsumption)` in cumulative extracted units `E`. So after extracting exactly `m_OreConsumption` units the deposit's concentration is `1/e` of its original — which is what "how much ore can be extracted before efficiency drops to 1/2.71th of the original" says (`ExtractorParameterPrefab.cs:17`, `:23`). A deposit therefore yields exponentially less rather than running to a hard zero on the curve — though the used counter is Euler-stepped and capped at 65535 rather than at `m_Base` (`AreaLotSimulationSystem.cs:826/834`), so one large step can overshoot and zero the cell (corrected 2026-08-10, the mechanical pass, from "never empties") — and because efficiency is `min(1, c / m_FullOre)` it stays at full efficiency until `c` falls below `m_FullOre` and only then starts dropping. That is the whole answer to "what does depletion do to a specialized industry's viability", and it is a formula rather than a threshold. `Rots:` the expression, since the round-trip through logs is exactly what a later rewrite simplifies away.
The same job also gates the whole write-down on `m_ExtractedAmount >= max(1, feature is Ore or Oil ? 1 : m_ResourceAmount * 0.001)` (`:699`), so a fertile or fish area only writes back once a thousandth of its stock has accumulated, while ore and oil write back at one unit.
**Forest is not in that switch at all** (`:690-697`): a forestry area is put on the same recompute list with no write-down (corrected 2026-08-10, the mechanical pass, from "a separate update list") and its wood comes from `AreaResourceSystem`'s tree pass (`m_MaxConcentration = max(..., woodAmount / m_WoodAmount)`, `AreaResourceSystem.cs:493-498`). So forestry depletion is tree state, which `environment-and-pollution` owns.
**Live evidence.** Both `Extractor` areas in the user's city are `MapFeature.FertileLand` with `m_RequireNaturalResource = true`, `m_WorkAmountFactor = 2`, `m_ObjectSpawnFactor = 2`, `m_MaxObjectArea = 0.35`: entity 288144 read `m_ResourceAmount = 161197.02`, `m_MaxConcentration = 0.9996946`, `m_ExtractedAmount = 120.45`, `m_WorkAmount = 1026.09`, `m_TotalExtracted = 1576.45`, `m_WorkType = Harvest`; entity 437039 read amount 66077.05, concentration 1.0, total extracted 704.90. Against a live `m_FullFertility = 0.5`, both give `min(1, 1.0/0.5) = 1` — full efficiency, so this city has no depletion to observe. Live `ExtractorParameterData`: fertility 0.1, fish 0.1, ore 500,000, forest 1, oil **250,000**, full fertility 0.5, full fish 0.5, full ore 0.8, full oil 0.8 — the oil figure is the one that differs from `ExtractorParameterPrefab`'s initializer of 100,000.

**Extractor production is priced as industrial and never as market.** `ExtractorCompanySystem` passes `isIndustrial: true` to both `GetCompanyProductionPerDay` and `GetCompanyProfitPerDay` (`:231`, `:298`), and the sector efficiency it picks up is `m_ExtractorProductionEfficiency` because `EconomyUtils.IsExtractorResource(output)` is checked first (`EconomyUtils.cs:1479`, the mask at `:1311-1314` decoding to Grain, Vegetables, Wood, Oil, Ore, Coal, Stone, Livestock, Cotton, Fish). It also records `CompanyStatisticData.m_LastUpdateProduce = produced * kCompanyUpdatesPerDay` (`:305-307`), which is the one field `CompanyEconomyStatisticSystem` reads back instead of recomputing, for extractors only (`CompanyEconomyStatisticSystem.cs:203`).
**`EconomyParameterData.m_ExtractorCompanyExportMultiplier` has no consumer.** Its tooltip promises "Export price multiplier for resources generated by an extractor company and sold to OC" (`EconomyPrefab.cs:13-14`), and a grep of `src/` finds it only in the prefab, the data struct and `EconomyParametersMode`. It is a struct field rather than a `const`, so the empty grep is real evidence rather than the inlining trap. **Two more economy parameters are dead the same way**: `m_ShopPossibilityIncreaseDivider` (tooltip: divides household resources to set shopping probability) and `m_PerOfficeResourceNeededForIndustrial` (tooltip: "The value of 2000 means that 2000 units of industrial resources produced affects the demand for offices by requiring 1 unit of office resources"). The live office-demand path is `m_OfficeResourceConsumedPerIndustrialUnit` in `OfficeAISystem` instead — see below. A `BuildingOption.Inactive` upgrade is skipped too (`:247-250`). So an extraction area attached to an unconnected upgrade contributes nothing and the hub's efficiency does not say so.

### Tourism: attractiveness, tourists, lodging and where the money lands

**Attractiveness is a saturating sum of squares over provider buildings.** `TourismSystem` (`SystemOrder.cs:413`, interval a flat **32768**, i.e. 8 passes a game day, `src/Game/Game.Simulation/TourismSystem.cs:156-159`) sums `m_Attractiveness²/10000` over every entity with `AttractivenessProvider`, then

```
attractiveness = 200 / (1 + exp(-0.3 * sum)) - 100
CityUtils.ApplyModifier(ref attractiveness, cityModifiers, CityModifierType.Attractiveness)
Tourism.m_Attractiveness = round(attractiveness)
```

(`:94-107`.) The sigmoid is bounded at 100 before the modifier, and every literal in it ships. **Squaring each provider before summing means one landmark beats many small parks of the same total** — that is the shape of the "attractions" mechanic the wiki describes qualitatively, expressed as arithmetic.
The per-building value is `AttractionSystem` (`SystemOrder.cs:551`, interval 16, `src/Game/Game.Simulation/AttractionSystem.cs:195-198`): start from the prefab's `AttractionData.m_Attractiveness`, add every installed upgrade's through `UpgradeUtils.CombineStats`, **multiply by the building's efficiency unless it is a `Signature` building** (`:102-106`), multiply by `0.8 + 0.2 * (Park.m_Maintenance / ParkData.m_MaintenancePool)` for a park (`:107-113`), and multiply by `1 + 0.01 * TerrainAttractivenessSystem.EvaluateAttractiveness(position, ...)` (`:114-118`). So a signature building's attractiveness ignores its own efficiency and everything else's does not, and park maintenance costs at most 20%.

**Tourist volume is a target curve plus a spawn probability plus a weather multiplier, all C#.**
`GetTargetTourists(attractiveness)` = `attractiveness * 15` up to 100, then `1500 + round(100 * log10(1 + (attractiveness - 100)))` (`TourismSystem.cs:171-180`) — **linear to 1500 tourists at attractiveness 100 and logarithmic above**, so attractiveness past 100 buys very little.
`GetSpawnProbability(attractiveness, currentTourists)` (`:182-197`): at or above target, the flat `GetRawTouristProbability = attractiveness / 1000` (`:166-169`); below half of `target * 1.1`, exactly 1; in between, `saturate(1.5 * (1 - (ratio - 0.5)/0.5)²)`.
`GetWeatherEffect` (`:204-232`) starts at 1 and adds a temperature term (lerping `m_TemperatureAffect.x` to 0 across the `m_AttractiveTemperature` band, or 0 to `.y` across 10 degrees past `m_ExtremeTemperature`), a snow or rain term across `m_SnowEffectRange` / `m_RainEffectRange`, and `m_SnowRainExtremeAffect.z` for a `Stormy` classification, then **clamps to `[0.5, 1.5]`**. `GetTouristProbability` is the product (`:199-202`). Every coefficient is `AttractivenessParameterData` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/AttractivenessParameterData.cs:6-29`); the clamp, the `/10`, the `/1000`, the `15`, the `1500` and the `1.1` are C# and ship.
`Tourism.m_AverageTourists` is written as `round(2 * GetTouristProbability(...) * 100000 / 16)` (`:108`) — a derived display figure, not a count. `Tourism { m_CurrentTourists, m_AverageTourists, m_Attractiveness, m_Lodging }` is the city-level singleton (`src/Game/Game.City/Tourism.cs:7-15`), `m_Lodging` an `int2` of (occupied rooms, total rooms) summed over hotels (`:81-93`). `TourismSystem.GetTouristRandomStay()` returns the literal 262144 — one game day (`:161-164`).
`citizens-and-households` owns the tourist household itself; `TouristSpawnSystem` rolls `GetTouristProbability` per pass.

**Lodging is a commercial resource sold room by room, and the hotel is a landlord.**
A hotel is any `ProcessingCompany` prefab whose output is `Lodging`, which is what adds `LodgingProvider` and a `Renter` buffer to its archetype (`ProcessingCompany.cs:36-40`). `LodgingProvider { m_FreeRooms, m_Price }` (`src/Game/Game.Companies/LodgingProvider.cs:6-10`).
`LodgingProviderSystem` (`SystemOrder.cs:464`, `kUpdatesPerDay = 32`, `src/Game/Game.Simulation/LodgingProviderSystem.cs:241`, `:259-262`) per pass:
`roomCount = (int)(lotSize.x * lotSize.y * level * BuildingPropertyData.m_SpaceMultiplier)` (`:264-267`) — so **rooms are the building's, not the company's**, and they scale linearly with level; evicts any renter that is not a `TouristHousehold` and any renter past the room count, clearing its `TouristHousehold.m_Hotel` (`:117-137`); charges each remaining tourist household `LeisureParametersData.m_TouristLodgingConsumePerDay / kUpdatesPerDay * GetMarketPrice(Lodging)` and credits the hotel the same total (`:138-150`); **decrements the hotel's `Lodging` stock and its `ServiceAvailable` by the room-nights consumed** (`:151-155`); and writes `m_Price = charge * kUpdatesPerDay` and `m_FreeRooms = roomCount - renters` (`:156-159`).
So a hotel with no `Lodging` stock still charges, but its `ServiceAvailable` floors at 0 and the commercial production taper then lets it restock. `CitizenPathfindSetup` is where a lodging seeker picks one: it skips a hotel with `m_FreeRooms == 0`, no property, or an inactive building, starts a qualifying hotel at −5000 against +5000 for every other target — the dominant term, missed by the original read and added 2026-08-10 by the mechanical pass — and inside that band scores by `-10 * m_FreeRooms` plus `min(m_Price, 500)`, minus a road-edge attractiveness term (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/CitizenPathfindSetup.cs:60-92`).
**Live**: the hotel at 54049 held `m_FreeRooms = 1075`, `m_Price = 282`, `ServiceAvailable = 5000` and a `Lodging` stock of 5086; the city read `Tourism { m_CurrentTourists = 30, m_AverageTourists = 100, m_Attractiveness = 8, m_Lodging = (5, 1080) }`. Five rooms occupied out of 1080 at attractiveness 8 — `GetTargetTourists(8) = 120`.

**Tourist money reaches the city as a statistic, not as a transfer.** `StatisticType.TouristIncome` is credited the household's starting money when a tourist household spawns (`src/Game/Game.Citizens/HouseholdInitializeSystem.cs:240-244`) and debited whatever money it still holds when it leaves (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/HouseholdMoveAwaySystem.cs:168-175`). So **tourist income is money brought in minus money taken out**, and everything a tourist spends in between reaches a company through the ordinary `ResourceBuyerSystem`, `LeisureSystem` and `LodgingProviderSystem` paths. That is the whole of "how tourism reaches commercial revenue": there is no tourist-specific revenue channel.
Starting money is `rand(m_TouristInitialWealthRange) - range/2 + m_TouristInitialWealthOffset` (`HouseholdInitializeSystem.cs:162`), and tourist consumption is multiplied by `m_TouristConsumptionMultiplier` (`HouseholdBehaviorSystem.cs:237-239`); `citizens-and-households` records both.
Verdict: **the wiki's `Tourism` page is correct on mechanism and is the only page in this topic with no staleness banner.** Its attractiveness-drives-volume claim, its dual role for a building's attractiveness value (city total *and* destination choice), its hotels-are-commercial-companies-selling-lodging claim and its attractions-override-distance claim all resolve in the decompile as cited above. It also carries a patch note the `Economy` page has not absorbed — that as of 1.3.3f1 commercial buildings selling Lodging, Recreation or Entertainment no longer gain the office efficiency bonus from airport and office signature buildings — which is consistent with `ProcessingCompanySystem.UpdateEfficiencyFactors` applying `CityModifierType.OfficeEfficiency` only when `!isCommercial` (`:280-288`). First-party wins as always, but this is a page that earned its agenda-setting.

### Demand is per resource, and the city's own demand figure is a projection over producers

`zoning-buildings-and-land-value` owns the three demand systems' outputs and their zone-level arithmetic. What this topic owns is the resource level.

**`CountCompanyDataSystem` is the seam** (`SystemOrder.cs:385`). Per resource it publishes twelve arrays through two structs, `CommercialCompanyDatas` and `IndustrialCompanyDatas` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/CountCompanyDataSystem.cs:27-57`), each handed out with an `out JobHandle` (`:559`, `:574`, `:588`, `:594`). `ProcessCompanies` (`:342-413`) walks every company with an `IndustrialProcessData` prefab and a `WorkProvider`, and for one with a property:

- adds `GetCompanyProductionPerDay(...)` to `m_Production[output]` (industrial) or `m_SalesCapacities[output]` (commercial);
- **adds the input consumption implied by that production to `m_Demand[input]`**, as `GetRoundedConsumption(inputAmount, outputAmount, production) = (inputAmount * production + outputAmount/2) / outputAmount` (`:369-376`, `:415-418`);
- adds `ServiceAvailable`/`m_MaxService` into `m_CurrentAvailables`/`m_TotalAvailables` for a commercial one;
- and counts a company with **no** property into `m_ProductionPropertyless` or `m_ServicePropertyless` (`:391-401`).

**So in-city demand for a resource is not consumption; it is the input requirement implied by every producer's *current production rate*.** `IndustrialAISystem` then hires and fires off `m_Demand[output] - m_Production[output]` (`IndustrialAISystem.cs:126-127`), and the propertyless counts are what `CommercialDemandSystem` and `IndustrialDemandSystem` use as the "companies exist with nowhere to go" signal `zoning-buildings-and-land-value` records.

**Company spawning is per resource and one-at-a-time.**
`CommercialSpawnSystem` (`SystemOrder.cs:368`) iterates every resource, skips it unless demand is positive and the last spawn for it is older than `DemandParameterData.m_FrameIntervalForSpawning.y`, **skips it if any propertyless commercial company already produces it**, and otherwise creates one entity from a uniformly random `CommercialCompanyData` prefab whose output masks the resource (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/CommercialSpawnSystem.cs:58-128`). The prefab set is `ArchetypeData` + `CommercialCompanyData` + `IndustrialProcessData` (`:179`); the propertyless set is `CommercialCompany` + `PrefabRef` excluding `PropertyRenter` (`:181`).
`IndustrialSpawnSystem` (`SystemOrder.cs:367`) is the same shape with three branches (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/IndustrialSpawnSystem.cs:126-178`): a produceable **non-material** resource spawns a factory if the roll `random.NextInt(round(5000 / min(5, max(1, log10(1 + population))))) < demand` passes and no existing industrial prefab already produces it; a produceable **material** resource spawns an extractor unconditionally when none exists, and `break`s out of the resource loop; and any **tradable** resource with positive warehouse demand spawns a warehouse. (Corrected 2026-08-10, the mechanical pass: the original said `m_EmptySignatureBuildingCount > 0` forces every branch — that field is read at `CommercialSpawnSystem.cs:65` and `IndustrialSpawnSystem.cs:139` and assigned nowhere in `src/`, so it is permanently 0 and the bypass cannot fire. Both systems are also gated whole: one frame in 128 each, and only while the matching demand sum is positive, `CommercialSpawnSystem.cs:196`, `IndustrialSpawnSystem.cs:264`.)
Note the asymmetry: the material branch's `break` means at most one extractor spawns per pass across all resources, while factories and warehouses can spawn several.

**The office half of the loop is one system and one parameter.** `ProcessingCompanySystem` counts, into a shared `NativeReference<int>`, every unit an industrial non-office company produced this tick (`ProcessingCompanySystem.cs:230-233`, the `!flag && !flag2` gate meaning not commercial and not on an office property). `OfficeAISystem` (`SystemOrder.cs:457`, `kUpdatesPerDay = 32`) then divides that total across the office companies and each sells `min(stock, ceil(total / officeCount) * m_OfficeResourceConsumedPerIndustrialUnit)` units at the industrial price straight into its own money, exporting the surplus above two thirds of `kMaxVirtualResourceStorage` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/OfficeAISystem.cs:112-132`, `kMinStorageAllow` and `kMinimumTradeResource = 2000` at `:164`).
So **an office company's customer is the industrial sector in aggregate, with no delivery, no pathfind and no per-company matching** — and the parameter that scales it is `m_OfficeResourceConsumedPerIndustrialUnit`, not the `m_PerOfficeResourceNeededForIndustrial` its own tooltip describes.

**Warehouses and outside connections are the same component with different neighbours.**
`TradeSystem` (`SystemOrder.cs:461`, `kUpdatesPerDay = 128`, interval `262144 / 128` with no `* 16`, `src/Game/Game.Simulation/TradeSystem.cs:259`, `:284-287`) queries `StorageCompany` + `Game.Objects.OutsideConnection` + `PrefabRef` + `Resources` + `TradeCost` (`:329`). Per pass it decays a per-resource `m_TradeBalances` array by `kRefreshRate = 0.01f` (`:115-116`, `:257`), recomputes the buy and sell cost for every resource × transfer type into a cache (`:120-133`), and then **drives each outside connection's stock toward half its capacity**: `target = limit / storedResourceCount` (or `GarbageFacilityData.m_GarbageCapacity` for garbage), `delta = target/2 - current`, `ratio = |delta / (target/2)|`, and the move is `delta` outright when the ratio exceeds 1, else `(int)(delta * ratio / kUpdatesPerDay) * 8` (`:158-186`). Each move is booked as `StatisticType.Trade` and into the import/export accumulator, **excluding the three mail resources** (the mask `28672` is `UnsortedMail | LocalMail | OutgoingMail`, `:186`).
`CalculateTradeCost` (`:345-366`) is the price of trading:

```
buy  = weightCost(type) * weight;  if (balance < 0) buy  *= 1 + distanceCost(type) * max(50, sqrt(-balance))
sell = weightCost(type) * weight;  if (balance > 0) sell *= 1 + distanceCost(type) * max(50, sqrt( balance))
```

with `CityModifierType.ImportCost` and `ExportCost` applied. So **the cost of importing rises with the square root of how much you already import**, floored at a `sqrt` of 50, and the two directions are independent. `GetBestCachedTradeCostAmongTypes` then takes the cheapest qualifying transfer mode per connection (`:85-107`), and `OutsideTradeParameterData.GetBestDistanceCostAmongTypes` / `GetBestWeightCostAmongTypes` do the same over the four real modes, masking with `0x17` to skip the unused bit (`OutsideTradeParameterData.cs:61-97`).
`TradeCost { m_Resource, m_BuyCost, m_SellCost, m_LastTransferRequestTime }` (`src/Game/Game.Companies/TradeCost.cs:7-15`) is a buffer on the company; `EconomyUtils.SetTradeCost` lerps a new value in with per-direction alphas and can preserve the timestamp (`EconomyUtils.cs:266-286`), and `GetLastTradeRequestTime` takes the max over the buffer (`:252-264`).

**A company restocks on a threshold, and the threshold divides the storage limit by a slot count.**
`BuyingCompanySystem` (`SystemOrder.cs:369`) computes a slot count of 1, 2 or 3 — 2 if the recipe has a second input, +1 if the output differs from input1 **and** has nonzero weight — and divides `StorageLimitData.m_Limit` by it (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/BuyingCompanySystem.cs:133-141`). `CalculateResourceNeeded` then flags a need when the on-hand amount, **plus every buying truck's load and every pending `Purpose.Shopping` trip's amount**, falls below `max(kResourceLowStockAmount, slotCapacity * 0.25f)` (`:213-241`). Constants at `:326-330`: `kNotificationCostLimit = 5f`, `kResourceLowStockAmount = 4000`, `kResourceMinimumRequestAmount = 2000`.
The request amount is `min(slotShare - onHand, min(storageLeft, truckCapacity))` where `slotShare` is `limit/3`, `limit/2` or `limit` depending on the same three cases, and the whole request is abandoned if it comes to `kResourceMinimumRequestAmount` or less (`:178-190`). A **weightless** input skips all of that and requests a flat `kResourceMinimumRequestAmount` (`:191-194`). The result is a `ResourceBuyer { m_Payer, m_AmountNeeded, m_Flags = Industrial | Import, m_Location, m_ResourceNeeded }` on the company (`:196-211`).
**The "no inputs" notification is priced, not stocked**: it fires when `GetTradeCost(resource, tradeCosts).m_BuyCost > kNotificationCostLimit` (`:220`), and the icon goes on the *property* rather than the company (`:150-172`). So a company with a full warehouse can show it, and one starving on a cheap route cannot.
Verdict: **the corpus's transcription of this threshold is version-stale, which is a useful demonstration that a forked vanilla body ages.** `ruzbeh0/Time2Work` ships an ILSpy transcription of the same job using `maxCapacity * 0.15f` (reported by the corpus sweep); 1.6.0f1 reads `0.25f` at `BuyingCompanySystem.cs:237`. First-party wins with no contest. Recorded because a reader who reaches for a corpus fork as a readable substitute for the decompile inherits whatever version it was forked at.

The total it reads is `ProcessingCompanySystem`'s `m_ProducedResources`, a persistent `NativeArray<long>` accumulated with `Interlocked.Add` in both `ProcessingCompanySystem.AddProducedResource` (`:351-364`) and `ExtractorCompanySystem.AddProducedResource` (`:398-411`).

**A tool-shaped limitation, recorded because it decided how this pass read 83 components and the next pass will hit it too.** The `unity` plugin's `eval` documents its exclusions as "lambdas, LINQ, loops, and control flow". - **`citizens-and-households`** — the other end of employment, exactly as that file's sixth bridge entry says. That topic produces a `Worker` with a level; this one consumes it as an `Employee` and turns happiness into production at `EconomyUtils.cs:1480`. Three things sit on the seam and are cited from both sides: `FreeWorkplaces.GetBestFor`, `CalculateNumberOfWorkplaces`, and the wage debit that `PayWageSystem` takes out of the company (`PayWageSystem.cs:120-127`). What this topic adds to that seam: happiness enters production **twice**, once through `GetWorkforce` and once through `EfficiencyFactor.EmployeeHappiness` (`WorkProviderSystem.cs:279-281`); `CompanyDividendSystem` moves a company's cash into its employees' households; and `ResourceBuyerSystem`'s sale is where a household's shopping money becomes a company's revenue. Household consumption, the shopping roll and the tourist household are that topic's.
- **`environment-and-pollution`** — owns the resource grid this topic's extraction economics run on. `NaturalResourceCell.m_Ore/.m_Oil/.m_Fertility/.m_Fish`, `AreaResourceSystem`'s recomputation of `Extractor.m_ResourceAmount` and `m_MaxConcentration`, and `AreaLotSimulationSystem.ExtractNaturalResources`'s write-down of the cells are all theirs; what this topic owns is `GetEffectiveConcentration` → `EfficiencyFactor.NaturalResources` → production → viability. The `CityModifierType.OreResourceAmount` and `OilResourceAmount` modifiers applied to a cell's base amount (`AreaLotSimulationSystem.cs:768`, `:773`) sit on that line. Forest is theirs entirely: it is absent from the depletion switch and its concentration comes from tree state (`AreaLotSimulationSystem.cs:690-697`, `AreaResourceSystem.cs:493-498`).
- **`city-services-and-coverage`** — owns most of the `EfficiencyFactor` writers this topic's production multiplies by (`ServiceBudget`, `ElectricitySupply`, `WaterSupply`, `Garbage`, `Telecom`, `Mail` and the rest), and owns the budget slider that feeds `ServiceBudget`: `CityServiceBudgetSystem.GetServiceEfficiency` evaluates `BuildingEfficiencyParameterData.m_ServiceBudgetEfficiencyFactor` as an `AnimationCurve1` over `budget / 100` (`CityServiceBudgetSystem.cs:1122-1125`), which is the non-linearity the wiki's `Services` page describes as "at 50% budget service runs at 25% efficiency". This topic owns the money side of the same slider: `ExpenseSource.ServiceUpkeep` is filled from `GetEstimatedServiceUpkeep`, which scales only the `Resource.Money` component of a building's upkeep by the budget percentage and leaves the material components alone (`:1127-1141`, the per-building collection at `:392-422`). Service fees, service trade and the five per-capita outside-service imports are shared: the rates and the flow are here, what the service does with them is there.
- **`city-state-and-progression`** — owns `CityModifier`, `Policy`, milestones and unlocks, and this topic reads all four. Every one of these expressions passes through `CityUtils.ApplyModifier`: `CityModifierType.Attractiveness` on the tourism sigmoid, `LoanInterest` on the loan rate, `ImportCost`/`ExportCost` on trade cost, `CityServiceImportCost` on the five per-capita imports, `CityServiceBuildingBaseUpkeepCost` on upkeep, `OfficeEfficiency`/`IndustrialEfficiency`/`OfficeSoftwareEfficiency`/`IndustrialElectronicsEfficiency`/`IndustrialFishInputEfficiency`/`IndustrialFishHubEfficiency` on production, `OreResourceAmount`/`OilResourceAmount` on deposits. `DistrictModifierType.LowCommercialTax` reaches only the commercial accrual site (`ServiceCompanySystem.cs:151`) and `ParkingFee` is where parking-fee income comes from. `Creditworthiness` is a sum of `MilestoneData.m_LoanLimit` (`MilestoneSystem.cs:106-108`), and `ProcessingRequirementData` is a production-driven unlock (`ProcessingRequirementSystem.cs:57-67`) with 45 live prefabs. The game-mode machinery is theirs and it rewrites this topic's parameters: `EconomyParametersMode`, `OutsideTradeParametersMode`, `ExtractorParametersMode`, `ServiceFeeParameterMode`, `AttractivenessParametersMode`, `DemandParameterMode` and `ProcessingCompanyGlobalMode` all exist in `src/Game/Game.Prefabs.Modes/`, and `ModeSettingData.m_TaxPaidMultiplier` scales collected tax (`TaxSystem.cs:250-254`).
- **`simulation-time-and-units`** — owns the frame arithmetic every rate here is expressed in: 262,144 frames per day, `SimulationUtils.GetUpdateFrame`, and the two `kUpdatesPerDay` conventions. `TourismSystem.GetTouristRandomStay()` returning the literal 262144 and `CompanyMoveAwaySystem`'s `65536`-frame insolvency window are both raw frame counts a reader has to convert.
- **`transportation-and-vehicles`** — owns the delivery half of every trade this topic prices. `DeliveryTruckSelectData.TrySelectItem` decides whether a shipment is worth a truck at all (`ProcessingCompanySystem.cs:264-273`, where the export gate is `item.m_Cost / min(stock, item.m_Capacity) < 0.03f`), and `DeliveryTruckAISystem` is where the money actually changes hands for a weighted resource (`:397`/`:401`). `EconomyUtils.GetTransportCost` has two overloads with different shapes — a per-shipment one at `distance * 0.03 * weight * (1 + floor(amount/1000))` (`EconomyUtils.cs:553-560`) and a per-mode one branching on `StorageTransferFlags` (`:562-573`) — and they are the join.
- **`binding-layer`** — not this topic's, but two economy facts live there and are recorded because a reader will hit them: `UIEconomyConfigurationPrefab`'s `m_IncomeItems`/`m_ExpenseItems` are the game's own grouping of the income and expense enums into panel rows, and `CityInfoUISystem.WriteDemandFactors` caps the demand-factor list at the top five after sorting.

## Dead ends

**The mod catalog sweep, and what it returned.** Read `plugins/cs2-modding/skills/cs2-modding-setup/references/mod-catalog.md` end to end and matched on the **Demonstrates** half. **No entry names economy material**, which the ticket predicted. The catalog was then ignored and all 22 repositories greped with 40 patterns; the results are in the corpus finding above. The dead end proper is that **the catalog could not have routed this pass**: the two repositories that mattered, `InfoLoom` and `Time2Work`, are catalogued for a citizen census and a citizen-behaviour substitution respectively, and `Tree_Controller`'s entry gives no hint that it forks the resource-area pipeline. The three contradicted entries and the six proposed sentences are in the catalog-gap finding above; what belongs here is the method note: **for a topic the catalog does not cover, the independent grep is the sweep and the catalog is a cross-check on it, not the other way round.** That is the reverse of the order the catalog's own instructions imply, and it cost nothing here only because the grep was run anyway.

**The install's packaged content was not opened, and the reason is the ruling rather than the cost.** `docs/SOURCES.md` entry 5 splits packaged prefabs by where they ship. Every resource prefab, every company prefab and every settings prefab in this topic is base-game, so it lives in `Cities2_Data/resources.assets` behind a Unity serialized-file parser rather than in a `Prefabs*.cok` zip. The running game answered every one of them in a single component read — 83 process prefabs, 41 resource prefabs, 36 taxable-resource masks, five parameter singletons and the city's four buffers — so the parser was never worth building. Under the ruling the values do not ship anyway. Anyone re-deriving these offline still faces that cost, and the content-pack route would only reach the DLC company prefabs.

**The compiled locale data and the UI bundle were not read.** The locale package would give the display names of the resources and the budget rows, which is `localization`'s surface rather than a mechanism; the UI bundle would give the Production tab's rendering, which is `binding-layer`'s. This topic's subject ships as C# and as prefab data, so `docs/SOURCES.md`'s precedence rule puts the decompile and the running game first and neither artifact adds anything. Recorded because the `Economy` page's three tables look like a bundle-side artifact and are not: they are 83 `IndustrialProcessData` components.

**The paused simulation is the one thing this pass could not work around, and it cost three questions.** The user's game was at `selectedSpeed == 0`. Every load-time value — the recipes, the resource flags, the taxable-resource masks, the parameter singletons, the tax array, the `ServiceFee` buffer, the mode list — is unaffected, because those are written at load. Three things could not be settled:
1. **Whether a commercial converter really accrues untaxed income twice per tick.** The method: arm no breakpoint, read one `Commercial_Restaurant` company's `TaxPayer.m_UntaxedIncome`, `advance` one `ProcessingCompanySystem` interval, read it again, and compare against the two systems' computed terms. Needs the simulation ticking.
2. **The size of the `CompanyProfitabilitySystem` valuation error.** The method: `eval` `EconomyUtils.GetCompanyTotalWorth` twice on the same commercial company, once with a default `IndustrialProcessData` and once with the prefab's, and difference them. This one needs no ticking and was simply not run; it is the cheapest outstanding item in this file.
3. **Whether `CompanyStatisticData`'s money fields are genuinely maintained.** Both sampled companies read all-zero for `m_Income`, `m_Worth`, `m_Profit`, `m_WagePaid`, `m_RentPaid`, `m_TaxPaid` and `m_CostBuyResource` despite `CompanyEconomyStatisticSystem` writing every one of them at 128 passes per entity per day.

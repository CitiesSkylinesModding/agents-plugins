## Findings

### The network is four entity kinds, and only two of them carry the topology

The four are the **node**, the **edge**, the **lane**, and the **composition prefab**; only the first two hold the graph, the third is where all behaviour lives, and the fourth is data rather than world state. `Game.Net` declares the topology in two components and nothing else. **`Edge { m_Start, m_End }`** (`src/Game/Game.Net/Edge.cs:6-10`) names the two node entities a segment joins; **`Node { m_Position, m_Rotation }`** (`src/Game/Game.Net/Node.cs:8-12`) is a float3 and a quaternion. The reverse references are buffers: `ConnectedEdge` on a node, `ConnectedNode` on an edge, both added by `NetPrefab.GetArchetypeComponents` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/NetPrefab.cs:18-29`).

An edge's shape is **`Curve { m_Bezier, m_Length }`** — a `Colossal.Mathematics.Bezier4x3`, four control points in 3-D (`src/Game/Game.Net/Curve.cs:9-13`). `m_Length` is **not serialized**: `Deserialize` recomputes it with `MathUtils.Length(m_Bezier)` (`:23`), so the bezier is the stored truth and the length is derived. Every position along an edge is a *curve position* in `[0,1]`, and that convention runs through the whole topic — lane objects, blockage ranges, path elements and parking slots are all expressed as curve positions rather than metres.

`EdgeGeometry { m_Start: Segment, m_End: Segment, m_Bounds }` (`src/Game/Game.Net/EdgeGeometry.cs:7-13`) is the *rendered* geometry, split into two `Segment`s each holding a left and a right bezier plus a `float2` length (`src/Game/Game.Net/Segment.cs:6-14`); `NodeGeometry` carries bounds, position, flatness and offset (`src/Game/Game.Net/NodeGeometry.cs:7-15`). `StartNodeGeometry` and `EndNodeGeometry` sit on the edge beside them (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/NetGeometryPrefab.cs:78-79`).

The third kind is the **lane**, a separate entity owned by an edge or a node through the `SubLane` buffer (`src/Game/Game.Net/SubLane.cs:4-7`, holding `m_SubLane` and the `PathMethod` mask it serves). A lane carries `Lane { m_StartNode, m_MiddleNode, m_EndNode }` — three `PathNode`s (`src/Game/Game.Net/Lane.cs:8-14`), which is the lane's identity *inside the pathfinding graph* rather than a geometric fact. The fourth is the **composition prefab**, which the next finding covers.

`Rots:` the component and field names throughout this file. Re-check against `src/Game/Game.Net/`, `src/Game/Game.Pathfind/`, `src/Game/Game.Prefabs/`, `src/Game/Game.Simulation/`, `src/Game/Game.Vehicles/`, `src/Game/Game.Citizens/`, `src/Game/Game.Objects/`, `src/Game/Game.Areas/`, `src/Game/Game.Tools/` and `src/Game/Game.Events/`, at the files cited beside each claim.

### `PathNode` is a packed 64-bit key, and it is the join between the world and the graph

`PathNode` wraps one `ulong m_SearchKey` (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathNode.cs:20`) laid out as: **owner entity index in the high 32 bits**, a 15-bit **curve position** at bits 16–30 (`curvePosition * 32767`, constants `FLOAT_TO_INT = 32767f` / `INT_TO_FLOAT = 3.051851E-05f` at `:10-12`), a **secondary-node bit** at bit 31 (`SECONDARY_NODE = 2147483648uL`, `:18`), and a 16-bit **lane index** in the low bits — which the four constructors alternately treat as one `ushort laneIndex` (`:32-40`) or as a `byte laneIndex` plus a `byte segmentIndex` (`:22-30`).

Two consequences a mod hits immediately. **The key stores an entity *index* and not its version** (`:24`, `:137-138`), so a `PathNode` does not survive entity recycling and `ReplaceOwner`/`SetOwner` exist to repoint one (`:90-101`). And **`EqualsIgnoreCurvePos` and `StripCurvePos` mask with `0xFFFFFFFF8000FFFF`** (`:69`, `:86`), which is how the graph treats "the same lane at a different point" as one node.

### Compositions: the cross-section is a prefab, and `Composition` is three references to one

`Game.Net.Composition { m_Edge, m_StartNode, m_EndNode }` (`src/Game/Game.Net/Composition.cs:6-12`) sits on an **edge entity** and holds three **entity references to composition prefabs** — not to the edge's own start and end nodes. `CompositionSelectSystem` fills all three by looking a composition up from the flag set computed for that edge and each of its ends (`src/Game/Game.Net/CompositionSelectSystem.cs:190-192`, `:220`), creating one on demand where none exists (`:1092-1103`).

`Verdict:` the wiki's `Roads` and `Traffic` pages carry no gloss of `Composition` at all, and the natural reading of the name — an edge together with its start and end node — is wrong. The decompile owns this and settles it three ways. `RoadSafetySystem` reads a road's flags with `m_RoadCompositionData.TryGetComponent(composition.m_Edge, …)` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/RoadSafetySystem.cs:98`), dereferencing `m_Edge` into a *prefab*. `GeometrySystem` indexes the other two the same way — `startCompositionData = m_PrefabCompositionData[composition.m_StartNode]` (`src/Game/Game.Net/GeometrySystem.cs:460`, again at `:872`), a **prefab** composition lookup keyed on a field named for a node. And `CompositionSelectSystem` is what put them there.

**The practical consequence is a trap.** A mod that adds `Updated` to `Composition.m_StartNode` believing it is marking the edge's start node has marked a *composition prefab* shared by every edge of that cross-section, and has not marked the node at all. The node is `Edge.m_Start`. One corpus mod's rebuild helper marks both, which is why it works (`RoadBuilder-CSII/RoadBuilder/Systems/RoadBuilderPrefabUpdateSystem.cs:125-156`).

**`NetCompositionData.m_HeightRange` is the field the collision pass reads**, and it is worth naming here because it is the lever a mod reaches for without realising it is a collision lever: `GeometrySystem` folds it into an edge's bounds as `value.m_Bounds.min.y += netCompositionData.m_HeightRange.min` and the matching `max` (`src/Game/Game.Net/GeometrySystem.cs:1762-1763`), and into a node's start and end bounds from each end's own composition (`:2961-2964`). Zeroing it on the prefab is therefore how a mod suppresses clearance detection without touching validation at all — which is what `Anarchy` does, recording the original on the composition entity and restoring it (`Anarchy/Anarchy/Systems/ClearanceViolation/ModifyNetCompositionDataSystem.cs:137-151`, restore at `Systems/ClearanceViolation/ResetNetCompositionDataSystem.cs:70-78`).

The flag set itself is `CompositionFlags`, a struct of three enums — one `General` and one `Side` per side (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/CompositionFlags.cs:6-84`). `General` has 24 members including `Node`, `Edge`, `Invert`, `Flip`, `DeadEnd`, `Intersection`, `Roundabout`, `LevelCrossing`, `Crosswalk`, `MedianBreak`, `TrafficLights`, `RemoveTrafficLights`, `AllWayStop`, `Elevated`, `Tunnel`, `Lighting`, `Gravel`, `Pavement`, `Tiles`, `WideMedian` and `StyleBreak` (`:9-42`). `Side` has 31 including `Raised`, `Lowered`, `PrimaryTrack`…`QuaternaryTrack`, `PrimaryStop`…`TertiaryStop`, `Sidewalk`, `WideSidewalk`, `ParkingSpaces`, `SoundBarrier`, `PrimaryLane`…`QuaternaryLane`, `ForbidLeftTurn`, `ForbidRightTurn`, `AddCrosswalk`, `RemoveCrosswalk`, `ForbidStraight`, `ForbidSecondary` and `ZonesDisabled` (`:45-78`). Four static masks partition them by what they mean — `nodeMask`, `optionMask`, `directionalMask` (`:98-102`) and the private `NODE_MASK_*`/`OPTION_MASK_*`/`DIRECTIONAL_MASK_*` constants they mirror (`:86-96`).

**This is where a road's per-segment visible and behavioural properties actually sit**, and it is the answer to "where do a road's visible properties live on the network model": the *prefab* carries what is true of the road type (`RoadData`, `NetGeometryData`), the *composition prefab* carries what is true of this cross-section variant (`NetCompositionData`, `RoadComposition`), and the *edge entity* carries only what is true of this one segment right now (`Road`, `NetCondition`, `LandValue`, `Composition`).

`Rots:` the two `CompositionFlags` enums are the fastest-moving surface in this topic — 55 members across the two, and the serializer already carries a `Version.compositionFlagRefactoring` branch that reconstructs pre-refactor saves by mapping two old bits onto `Side.PrimaryTrack` and nothing else (`:116-134`). Re-check member names and values against `src/Game/Game.Prefabs/CompositionFlags.cs`.

### The lane hierarchy: the pathfinder plans on master lanes and the driver picks a slave lane

A multi-lane carriageway is represented twice. The **slave lanes** are the physical lanes a vehicle drives on, each carrying `SlaveLane { m_Flags, m_Group, m_MinIndex, m_MaxIndex, m_SubIndex, m_MasterIndex }` (`src/Game/Game.Net/SlaveLane.cs`), whose `SlaveLaneFlags` describe how the lane behaves in the group — `AllowChange`, `StartingLane`, `EndingLane`, `MultipleLanes`, `MergingLane`, `OpenStartLeft`/`Right`, `OpenEndLeft`/`Right`, `SplitLeft`/`Right`, `MiddleStart`, `MiddleEnd`, `MergeLeft`, `MergeRight` (`src/Game/Game.Net/SlaveLaneFlags.cs:4-19`). The **master lane** stands for the whole group: `MasterLane { m_Flags, m_Group, m_MinIndex, m_MaxIndex }` (`src/Game/Game.Net/MasterLane.cs`), where `m_MinIndex`/`m_MaxIndex` index into the owner's `SubLane` buffer.

**Only the master lane becomes a pathfinding edge.** `LanesModifiedSystem`'s created- and updated-lane queries both carry `None = { …, SlaveLane }` (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/LanesModifiedSystem.cs:1052`, `:1083`), so a slave lane never produces a `CreateAction` and never appears in the graph.

**The concrete lane is chosen at drive time.** `CarNavigationSystem.GetSlaveLaneFromMasterLane` resolves a master lane to one of `lanes[m_MinIndex … m_MaxIndex]` either by `NetUtils.ChooseClosestLane` against the next target position (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/CarNavigationSystem.cs:1299`, `:1311`) or, when there is no position to aim at, by `GetRandomLane` — reservoir sampling over the lanes of the group whose `SubLane.m_PathMethods` admit the vehicle's mode, falling back to a uniform index when none does (`:1336-1353`). `UpdateSlaveLane` re-picks the closest lane as a target position becomes known and stamps `CarLaneFlags.FixedLane` (`:1274-1285`).

So **the path is a sequence of carriageways, not of lanes**, and lane choice is a local decision made per navigation step. A mod that wants to control which lane a vehicle uses is not editing the path; it is editing this resolution or the `SubLane.m_PathMethods` masks it filters on.

The remaining lane roles are separate components on the same entity kind: `SecondaryLane` is a bare tag (`src/Game/Game.Net/SecondaryLane.cs`); `EdgeLane { m_EdgeDelta, m_ConnectedStartCount, m_ConnectedEndCount }` says which stretch of the parent edge a lane spans, with a serializer that compresses the six canonical delta pairs to one byte (`src/Game/Game.Net/EdgeLane.cs`); `NodeLane { m_WidthOffset, m_Flags, m_SharedStartCount, m_SharedEndCount }` is the node-side counterpart (`src/Game/Game.Net/NodeLane.cs`).

### The five lane payload components, and the flag sets that drive everything

A lane entity carries a payload component naming what it is, and each payload's flag enum is where the behaviour lives.

- **`CarLane`** (`src/Game/Game.Net/CarLane.cs:7-31`): `m_AccessRestriction` (an entity), `m_Flags`, `m_DefaultSpeedLimit`, `m_SpeedLimit`, `m_Curviness`, `m_CarriagewayGroup`, `m_BlockageStart`/`m_BlockageEnd`, `m_CautionStart`/`m_CautionEnd`, `m_FlowOffset`, `m_LaneCrossCount`. The four `byte` range fields are curve positions scaled by 255 and read back through the `blockageBounds`/`cautionBounds` properties, which multiply by `0.003921569f` (= 1/255) (`:33-35`).
- **`CarLaneFlags`**, a `uint` with 32 members and every bit used (`src/Game/Game.Net/CarLaneFlags.cs:6-39`): `Unsafe`, `UTurnLeft`, `Invert`, `SideConnection`, `TurnLeft`, `TurnRight`, `LevelCrossing`, `Twoway`, `IsSecured`, `Runway`, `Yield`, `Stop`, `SecondaryStart`, `SecondaryEnd`, `ForbidBicycles`, `PublicOnly`, `Highway`, `UTurnRight`, `GentleTurnLeft`, `GentleTurnRight`, `Forward`, `Approach`, `Roundabout`, `RightLimit`, `LeftLimit`, `ForbidPassing`, `RightOfWay`, `TrafficLights`, `ParkingLeft`, `ParkingRight`, `Forbidden`, `AllowEnter`.
- **`PedestrianLane`**, with `PedestrianLaneFlags` = `Unsafe`, `Crosswalk`, `AllowMiddle`, `AllowEnter`, `SideConnection`, `ForbidTransitTraffic`, `OnWater`, `AllowExit`, `AllowBicycle` (`src/Game/Game.Net/PedestrianLaneFlags.cs:4-15`).
- **`ParkingLane`** (`src/Game/Game.Net/ParkingLane.cs`): `m_AccessRestriction`, `m_AdditionalStartNode`, `m_Flags`, `m_FreeSpace`, `m_ParkingFee`, `m_ComfortFactor`, `m_TaxiAvailability`, `m_TaxiFee` — the last four all `ushort`. `ParkingLaneFlags` has 19 members (`src/Game/Game.Net/ParkingLaneFlags.cs:4-22`).
- **`ConnectionLane`**, with `ConnectionLaneFlags` = `Start`, `Distance`, `Outside`, `SecondaryStart`, `SecondaryEnd`, `Road`, `Track`, `Pedestrian`, `Parking`, `AllowMiddle`, `AllowCargo`, `Airway`, `Inside`, `Area`, `Disabled`, `AllowEnter`, `AllowExit`, `NoRestriction` (`src/Game/Game.Net/ConnectionLaneFlags.cs:4-23`). This is the lane kind that joins the network to buildings, outside connections, parking facilities and areas.
- **`TrackLane`** for rail, tram and subway, plus `GarageLane { m_ParkingFee, m_ComfortFactor, m_VehicleCount, m_VehicleCapacity }` for a parking structure's aggregate capacity (`src/Game/Game.Net/GarageLane.cs`).

`LaneObject` is the buffer that ties moving and parked objects to a lane: `{ m_LaneObject, m_CurvePosition }`, `IComparable` on `m_CurvePosition.x` so the buffer stays sorted along the lane (`src/Game/Game.Net/LaneObject.cs`). It is `IEmptySerializable` — **the buffer is rebuilt on load, not saved**.

### Road classes: what the taxonomy is, and what actually differs

The prefab hierarchy is `PrefabBase` → `NetPrefab` → `NetGeometryPrefab` → the class prefab. Exactly **nine** classes subclass `NetGeometryPrefab` and no other class subclasses `NetPrefab`: `RoadPrefab`, `TrackPrefab`, `PathwayPrefab`, `TaxiwayPrefab`, `WaterwayPrefab`, `PipelinePrefab`, `PowerLinePrefab`, `NetFencePrefab` and `FencePrefab` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/RoadPrefab.cs:14`, `TrackPrefab.cs:11`, `PathwayPrefab.cs:10`, `TaxiwayPrefab.cs:10`, `WaterwayPrefab.cs:10`, `PipelinePrefab.cs:9`, `PowerLinePrefab.cs:9`, `NetFencePrefab.cs:8`, `FencePrefab.cs:8`; the parent chain at `NetGeometryPrefab.cs:12` and `NetPrefab.cs:10`). That set is a C# fact, offline-checkable, and it is the class taxonomy the reference should carry.

**`RoadPrefab` adds exactly five authored fields**: `m_RoadType` (a two-member enum, `Normal` or `PublicTransport` — `src/Game/Game.Prefabs/RoadType.cs:3-7`), `m_SpeedLimit`, `m_ZoneBlock`, `m_TrafficLights`, `m_HighwayRules` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/RoadPrefab.cs:16-24`). Those five become `RoadData { m_ZoneBlockPrefab, m_SpeedLimit, m_Flags }` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/RoadData.cs:6-12`) plus geometry-flag edits, and the conversion is in one readable block of `NetInitializeSystem`:

- **`m_SpeedLimit` is authored in km/h and stored in m/s**: `value23.m_SpeedLimit = prefab10.m_SpeedLimit / 3.6f` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/NetInitializeSystem.cs:1944`). Four sibling net classes do the same at `:2025`, `:2054`, `:2100`, `:2136`. A reader who queries `RoadData.m_SpeedLimit` and expects the number the wiki tabulates is out by 3.6.
- **`m_ZoneBlock != null`** sets `GeometryFlags.SnapCellSize`, stores the zone-block prefab entity and sets `RoadFlags.EnableZoning` (`:1945-1950`).
- **`m_TrafficLights`** sets `RoadFlags.PreferTrafficLights` (`:1951-1954`).
- **`m_HighwayRules`** sets `RoadFlags.UseHighwayRules`, raises `m_MinNodeOffset` to at least 2 and adds `GeometryFlags.SmoothElevation` (`:1955-1960`).

`Game.Prefabs.RoadFlags` is the seven-member result: `EnableZoning`, `SeparatedCarriageways`, `PreferTrafficLights`, `DefaultIsForward`, `UseHighwayRules`, `DefaultIsBackward`, `HasStreetLights` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/RoadFlags.cs:6-15`). **It is a different enum from `Game.Net.RoadFlags`**, which has five members and lives on the runtime edge: `StartHalfAligned`, `EndHalfAligned`, `IsLit`, `AlwaysLit`, `LightsOff` (`src/Game/Game.Net/RoadFlags.cs:6-13`). Both are named `RoadFlags`, and both are tested **on the same line** of `RoadSafetySystem` — `(componentData.m_Flags & Game.Prefabs.RoadFlags.HasStreetLights) != 0 && (road.m_Flags & Game.Net.RoadFlags.LightsOff) == 0` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/RoadSafetySystem.cs:103`). The decompile disambiguates them with fully-qualified names throughout — a reader who imports the wrong one gets a compile error at best and a silently wrong bit test at worst.

Two of the seven flags are **not** authored on the road prefab and are derived per composition instead: `SeparatedCarriageways` from `CompositionState.SeparatedCarriageways` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/NetCompositionSystem.cs:226-229`, set at `src/Game/Game.Prefabs/NetCompositionHelpers.cs:1413`), and `HasStreetLights` from the composition carrying **any** `NetCompositionObject` whose prefab has `StreetLightData` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/NetCompositionSystem.cs:263-275`). ### Yield, stop and right-of-way come out of one derived priority number

`RoadComposition.m_Priority` starts as the road's own speed limit, loses `1.25` if the composition has `CompositionFlags.General.Gravel`, and gains a lane-count term — `max(forwardLanes, backwardLanes)` under highway rules, and `max(fwd,bwd) * 0.5 + (fwd+bwd) * 0.25` otherwise, counting only `NetCompositionLane`s flagged `Road` and not `Master` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/NetCompositionSystem.cs:224-262`).

`LaneSystem.CalculateYieldOffset` then turns that number into a three-way decision for each arm of a junction (`src/Game/Game.Net/LaneSystem.cs:5554-5621`):

1. `CompositionFlags.General.AllWayStop` on the node composition → **2** (stop), short-circuiting everything (`:5557-5560`).
2. `LevelCrossing` or `TrafficLights` → **0** (no yield): a signalled junction never assigns priority (`:5561-5564`).
3. Otherwise, scan the other arms for one whose `m_Priority` exceeds this arm's **by more than 0.99**. Two or more such arms → **1** (yield) at once. Exactly one → check the targets too, and yield if a target arm also outranks this one (`:5565-5577`, `:5612-5619`).
4. With no higher-priority arm and `RoadFlags.UseHighwayRules` set, a separate branch counts arms that have any non-turning or gentle-turning movement; the source's own arm having one returns 0, and otherwise one or more such arms returns 1 (`:5580-5610`).

`CreateNodeCarLanes` maps the yield value onto lane flags: `1 → CarLaneFlags.Yield`, `2 → CarLaneFlags.Stop`, `-1 → CarLaneFlags.RightOfWay` (`:7114-7128`), and `CompositionFlags.General.TrafficLights` on the intersection separately adds `CarLaneFlags.TrafficLights` (`:7130-7131`). `CalculateYieldOffset` itself returns only 0, 1 and 2 — the `-1` arm is fed by roundabout lane creation, which passes `math.select(0, -1, totalSourceCount >= 2)` to the exiting arm's lanes (`:1565`, consumed at `:1621`/`:1649` whose targets are the exit positions); the circulating arcs are passed a literal 0. A side connection into the middle of an edge always yields when it is the source (`:3436`).

**The threshold is the mechanism to teach.** Because the comparison is `> 0.99` and the priority is a speed limit in m/s plus a small lane term, two roads of the same class never assign priority to each other, and a difference of one class step almost always does. That is why an unsignalled junction of two identical roads behaves as an all-way give-way and a junction of a large road with an alley does not.

### The four pathfinding weights, and the one expression that uses them

`PathfindWeights` is a `float4` in a positional constructor `(time, behaviour, money, comfort)`, with `time => m_Value.x` and `money => m_Value.z` exposed as properties and the other two reachable only positionally (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathfindWeights.cs:5-11`). `PathfindCosts` is the same shape with the same field order (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathfindCosts.cs:5-7`), and the authoring struct `PathfindCostInfo` names them in full — `m_Time`, `m_Behaviour`, `m_Money`, `m_Comfort` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/PathfindCostInfo.cs:7-15`). **So the four weights are time, behaviour, money and comfort, in that order, and `.y` is behaviour and `.w` is comfort.**

The cost of traversing one edge is a single dot product (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathfindJobs.cs:641-655`):

```
speed  = PathUtils.CalculateSpeed(spec, parameters)
Δ      = delta.y - delta.x
value  = spec.m_Costs.m_Value                                   // baked per-edge costs
value.xyw += spec.m_Length * float3(1/speed,
                                    rules has any Forbid* ? 1 : 0,
                                    rules has AvoidBicycles ? 0.1 : 0)
value.y   += (RequireAuthorization ≠ authorization matches) ? 100 : 0
value.xyz  = 0  when the edge is free in the travelled direction, or Boarding-only
return dot(value, weights.m_Value) * abs(Δ)
```

Everything about the model is in those six lines. **Time accrues as `length / speed`. Behaviour accrues as `length` per metre on any edge carrying a rule the agent is not allowed to ignore, plus a flat 100 for an authorization mismatch. Comfort accrues as `length * 0.1` for `AvoidBicycles` only. Money never accrues here at all** — the money component comes entirely from the edge's pre-baked `m_Costs`. And the whole thing scales by the fraction of the edge actually traversed.

`PathUtils.CalculateCost` is the simplified sibling used outside the search, and it drops the behaviour/comfort accruals entirely, keeping `value.x += length / speed` and the same dot product, then multiplying by `random.NextFloat(0.5f, 1f)` unless `PathfindFlags.Stable` is set (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathUtils.cs:28-35`).

`RuleFlags` is the seven-member set the behaviour term keys on: `HasBlockage`, `ForbidCombustionEngines`, `ForbidTransitTraffic`, `ForbidHeavyTraffic`, `ForbidPrivateTraffic`, `ForbidSlowTraffic`, `AvoidBicycles` (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/RuleFlags.cs:6-15`). An agent's `PathfindParameters.m_IgnoredRules` is masked out of the edge's rules before the cost is computed, with `m_TaxiIgnoredRules` substituted on any edge whose methods include `PathMethod.Taxi` (`PathfindJobs.cs:610`).

`PathMethod` is the 16-member mode mask: `Pedestrian`, `Road`, `Parking`, `PublicTransportDay`, `Track`, `Taxi`, `CargoTransport`, `CargoLoading`, `Flying`, `PublicTransportNight`, `Boarding`, `Offroad`, `SpecialParking`, `MediumRoad`, `Bicycle`, `BicycleParking` (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathMethod.cs:6-23`).

### How the weights vary by demographic — the wiki is wrong about this

**`Verdict:`** the wiki's `Traffic` page states that Time is "the foremost factor for Adult citizens", Comfort "the foremost factor for Senior citizens", and Money "the foremost factor for Teen citizens" (https://cs2.paradoxwikis.com/Traffic, section flagged "outdated info… last verified for version pre-release"). **The decompile does not do this at 1.6.0f1, and there is no age branch anywhere in the weight computation.** `CitizenUtils.GetPathfindWeights` is the only computation of a citizen's weights and takes `(Citizen citizen, Household household, int householdCitizens)` (`src/Game/Game.Citizens/CitizenUtils.cs:81-89`) — no `CitizenAge`, no call to `citizen.GetAge()`, and every one of its **eleven call sites** passes the same three arguments (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/FindJobSystem.cs:201`, `FindSchoolSystem.cs:109`, `HouseholdFindPropertySystem.cs:392`/`:398`, `LeisureSystem.cs:593`, `PersonalCarAISystem.cs:825`, `ResidentAISystem.cs:3102`, `TaxiAISystem.cs:736`, `TripNeededSystem.cs:1079`/`:1400`, `ResourceBuyerSystem.cs:772`). Four of those sites overwrite the result with a literal profile when the citizen's purpose is `Purpose.EmergencyShelter` — an override, not a second computation, recorded at the non-citizen profiles below. The decompile owns anything C# names, so it wins without argument. What cannot be established either way is whether the wiki was ever right: its section is stamped pre-release, and no source here reaches a pre-release build to say whether age branching existed and was removed or was only ever described.

What actually varies, in full (`CitizenUtils.cs:83-88`):

```
time      = 5 * (4 - 3.75 * m_LeisureCounter / 255)
behaviour = 2                                                   // constant, every citizen
money     = 2500 * max(1, householdCitizens) / max(250, household.m_ConsumptionPerDay)
comfort   = 1 + 2 * citizen.GetPseudoRandom(CitizenPseudoRandom.TrafficComfort).NextFloat()
money    *= 0.1   when the household is not MovedIn and the citizen is not
                  MovingAwayReachOC / Tourist / Commuter
```

So the four axes of variation are **leisure satisfaction, household size against household consumption, a stable per-citizen random draw, and house-hunting status** — not age. Reading each:

- **Time is driven by leisure debt.** `m_LeisureCounter` is a `byte` on `Citizen` (`src/Game/Game.Citizens/Citizen.cs:19`) that rises when a citizen takes leisure (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/LeisureSystem.cs:76`) and falls each happiness tick (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/CitizenHappinessSystem.cs:500`). At 0 the time weight is 20, at 255 it is 1.25 — **a leisure-starved citizen is the one in a hurry**, which is the opposite of the intuitive reading. `CitizenInitializeSystem` seeds it at `random.NextInt(128)` or `random.NextInt(92) + 128` depending on branch (`src/Game/Game.Citizens/CitizenInitializeSystem.cs:97`/`:101`).
- **Money is per-capita consumption, inverted.** A household with high consumption per head gets a *small* money weight; the `max(250, …)` floor caps the weight at 10 per household member. And the tenfold reduction while house-hunting is why a household choosing a home barely weighs travel cost.
- **Comfort is fixed per citizen for life**, drawn from the `CitizenPseudoRandom.TrafficComfort` stream (`src/Game/Game.Citizens/CitizenPseudoRandom.cs`, value `305419905u`), giving a stable value in `[1,3]`.
- **Behaviour is the same 2 for every citizen**, which makes the *other* profiles below the whole of the game's behaviour modelling.

`Unconfirmed:` whether age correlates with any of the four inputs indirectly — whether, say, seniors systematically carry a different `m_LeisureCounter` distribution. Settling it is a live sweep: `ecs_query` on `Game.Citizens.Citizen` and a batched read of `m_LeisureCounter` bucketed by `GetAge()`. Not run.

### The non-citizen weight profiles are where behaviour modelling actually lives

Every non-citizen pathfind request sets its weights literally, and the census over the decompile is short enough to state as a shape. Three profiles account for nearly all of them, plus one single-site oddity: `TouristFindTargetSystem` requests a tourist's attraction target at `(0.1, 0.1, 0.1, 0.2)` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/TouristFindTargetSystem.cs:86`):

- **`(1,1,1,1)` — the neutral profile**, used by every routine service and dispatch: garbage, mail, maintenance, police patrol, hearse, goods delivery, prisoner transport, transport-vehicle dispatch, random traffic, route pathfinding and area-lot simulation (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/GarbageCollectorDispatchSystem.cs:336`, `PostVanDispatchSystem.cs:373`, `MaintenanceVehicleDispatchSystem.cs:438`, `PolicePatrolDispatchSystem.cs:339`, `HearseAISystem.cs:470`, `GoodsDeliveryDispatchSystem.cs:217`, `PrisonerTransportDispatchSystem.cs:327`, `RandomTrafficDispatchSystem.cs:221`, `AreaLotSimulationSystem.cs:503`, `src/Game/Game.Routes/RoutePathSystem.cs:560`, and others).
- **`(1,0,0,0)` — the emergency profile**: time only, with behaviour, money and comfort **zeroed**. Used by fire rescue and police-emergency dispatch, the ambulance half of healthcare dispatch (the hearse half is neutral, `HealthcareDispatchSystem.cs:507`/`:600`), fire engines, police cars, ambulances and their aircraft on the urgent leg — outbound, or an ambulance carrying a critical patient in (`AmbulanceAISystem.cs:523`) — and a citizen's own `Purpose.EmergencyShelter` trip (`TripNeededSystem.cs:1431`) (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/FireRescueDispatchSystem.cs:405`/`:437`, `PoliceEmergencyDispatchSystem.cs:391`/`:430`, `HealthcareDispatchSystem.cs:477`/`:544`, `FireEngineAISystem.cs:602`, `PoliceCarAISystem.cs:499`, `AmbulanceAISystem.cs:525`, `FireAircraftAISystem.cs:516`, `MedicalAircraftAISystem.cs:487`, `PoliceAircraftAISystem.cs:672`).
- **`(1,0.2,0,0.1)` — the evacuation/urgent profile**, used by evacuation dispatch, an evacuating bus (`TransportCarAISystem.cs:611`), and as a `Purpose.EmergencyShelter` override written **after** `GetPathfindWeights` on a walking resident, a personal car and a taxi occupant (`EvacuationDispatchSystem.cs:328`/`:357`, `ResidentAISystem.cs:3176`, `PersonalCarAISystem.cs:829`, `TaxiAISystem.cs:779`). The no-household fallback in `PersonalCarAISystem` is the **neutral** literal in its job initializer (`:761`), not this profile. The same purpose gets `(1,0,0,0)` at `TripNeededSystem.cs:1431` — the game's one inconsistency across the four override sites.

**The emergency profile is the whole of "emergency vehicles may make dangerous decisions", and it works because of where the costs sit.** Every U-turn, forbidden-lane, turning, lane-cross and curve penalty lives in the behaviour, money and comfort components and **almost none of it in time** (see the live table above: `m_UTurnCost` and `m_UnsafeUTurnCost` are `(0, y, 0, w)`, `m_TurningCost` and `m_LaneCrossCost` are comfort-only). Multiplying by `(1,0,0,0)` therefore makes every one of those penalties cost exactly nothing, and the vehicle takes the time-optimal route through any manoeuvre. Beside it, the **dispatch** sites set `m_IgnoredRules` to the union of all six restriction rules (`FireRescueDispatchSystem.cs:406`, `PoliceEmergencyDispatchSystem.cs:392`, `HealthcareDispatchSystem.cs:478`), which strips the behaviour-term rule accrual too; the vehicles' own AI systems ignore smaller, prefab-dependent sets instead — `MedicalAircraftAISystem.cs:488` assigns one rule, `FireEngineAISystem.cs:585`/`:603` and `AmbulanceAISystem.cs:500`/`:526` compose two onto a base including `VehicleUtils.GetIgnoredPathfindRules(carData)`, and `PoliceCarAISystem.cs:477` sets the six-rule union in its initializer for both the emergency and the returning branch. Returning to base, the same vehicle switches back to `(1,1,1,1)` (`PoliceCarAISystem.cs:503`, `FireEngineAISystem.cs:608`, `AmbulanceAISystem.cs:495`).

`Verdict:` the wiki's behaviour claim — "emergency vehicles have a more lenient behavior model… able to make illegal U-turns if it shortens their route" (https://cs2.paradoxwikis.com/Traffic) — is **confirmed** by the decompile, and the decompile supplies the mechanism the wiki lacks: it is a zeroed weight vector plus a full `m_IgnoredRules` mask, not a separate model.

**Freight is the fourth profile and it is computed rather than literal.** `ResourceBuyerSystem` sets `new PathfindWeights(0.01f, 0.01f, transportCost, 0.01f)` where `transportCost = EconomyUtils.GetTransportCost(1f, amount, resourceWeight, StorageTransferFlags.Car)` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/ResourceBuyerSystem.cs:883-888`). Time, behaviour and comfort are pinned at 0.01 and **the money weight is the actual cost of hauling this load**, so a heavier or larger consignment makes distance dominate the route choice proportionally. `Verdict:` this confirms the wiki's "the cost of resources they transport increases the further away they are delivered… selling resources and goods locally is more cost-efficient" (https://cs2.paradoxwikis.com/Traffic), and again supplies the mechanism: the load's transport cost *is* the money weight, multiplying a money cost component that accrues per metre through `PathfindCarData.m_DrivingCost` (live `(0,0,0.01,0)` — money-only).

### Two different ceilings on what a trip may cost

A search abandons when its running total passes `PathfindParameters.m_MaxCost`, which `PathfindJobs` reads once into `m_MaxTotalCost = m_MaxCost - m_CostOffset`, treating **0 as "no limit"** (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathfindJobs.cs:368`). Two ceilings feed it:

- **A C# constant for the driving and resident paths.** `CitizenBehaviorSystem.kMaxPathfindCost` is `public static readonly float = 17000f` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/CitizenBehaviorSystem.cs:982`), used by `PersonalCarAISystem.cs:799`, `ResidentAISystem.cs:3071` and `TaxiAISystem.cs:708`. This **is** a C# constant under the ruling and ships as a number.
- **A per-purpose ceiling derived from a prefab.** `TripPriorityParametersData.GetMaxCost(byte priority)` returns `m_BaseMaxCost * (priority / 128f)` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/TripPriorityParametersData.cs:83-86`), and `GetPriority(Purpose, Citizen)` maps each `Purpose` to one of twelve priority bytes, with **`Purpose.Leisure` alone interpolated**: `round(lerp(m_LeisurePriorityMin, m_LeisurePriorityMax, 1 - m_LeisureCounter/255))` (`:73-77`). The formula ships whole; the bytes do not.

Five call sites use the derived ceiling, and two of them multiply by `1.1`: going to work and going to school (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/FindJobSystem.cs:203`, `FindSchoolSystem.cs:111`), against plain `GetMaxCost` for going home (`HouseholdFindPropertySystem.cs:408`), shopping (`ResourceBuyerSystem.cs:775`), leisure (`LeisureSystem.cs:596`) and the general trip path (`TripNeededSystem.cs:1082`, `:1403`).

Read live at 1.6.0f1 the singleton (one entity, prefab `TripPriorityParameters`) carries `m_BaseMaxCost = 8000`, and priorities: home 192, work 96, school 96, shopping 64, hospital 128, safety 128, escape 128, emergency shelter 128, deathcare 128, moving away 192, tourist 64, criminal 128, leisure min 32 / max 96. Those give per-purpose ceilings of 12000 home, 6000 work and school (6600 with the 1.1), 4000 shopping, 4000 tourist, 8000 for the 128-priority purposes, 12000 moving away, and 2000–6000 for leisure. These numbers stay in this file; the reference names the component, the two methods, and `GetSingleton<TripPriorityParametersData>` as the read.

**The consequence worth teaching is the asymmetry**: going home is allowed to cost three times what shopping may, so a city that has become expensive to cross loses shopping and leisure trips first and commuting last. That is a direction, not a ratio — the numbers behind it do not ship.

### Route selection: A*, an admissible heuristic, and deliberate randomisation

The search is a min-heap A* over the pathfind graph (`UnsafeMinHeap<HeapData>` sized 1000, node data 10000, both in the per-thread linear allocator — `src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathfindJobs.cs:264-267`). Three things about it change what a mod can do.

**The heuristic is the cheapest possible per-metre cost across the modes in play.** `Initialize` starts `m_HeuristicCostFactor` at `1000000f` and takes the componentwise minimum over each enabled `PathMethod`'s cost profile dotted with the agent's own weights — pedestrian, car, track, flying, off-road, taxi, passenger transport, cargo transport (`:199-247`). The per-mode profiles come from `NetInitializeSystem`, which seeds them at `1000000f` and reduces them to the **minimum over every registered pathfind prefab's `m_DrivingCost` / `m_WalkingCost`** (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/NetInitializeSystem.cs:2569-2573`, `:363`, `:376`), handed to the queue through `GetHeuristicData()` (`:689-692`). `CalculateTotalCost` then adds `distance-to-goal-bounds * m_HeuristicCostFactor` (`PathfindJobs.cs:658-663`). **Adding a cheaper net prefab therefore lowers the heuristic for every agent in the game**, widening every search.

**`PathfindFlags.NoHeuristics` turns A* into Dijkstra** by zeroing the factor (`:193-196`); `PathfindFlags.Stable` absent **doubles** it (`:248-251`), which makes the search greedier and less exact; and `PathfindFlags.ParkingReset` sets `m_ReducedCostFactor = 0.5f`, halving both the heuristic and the cost of parking-method edges (`:188-192`, `:674-677`).

**Route variation is a deliberate per-edge random multiplier, not an emergent effect.** In `AddConnections`, `num2 = costFactor * select(m_Random.NextFloat(0.5f, 1f), 1f, Stable)` (`PathfindJobs.cs:687`) — so unless `PathfindFlags.Stable` is set, **every edge's cost is multiplied by a fresh uniform draw in [0.5, 1]**, and two identical agents on identical journeys take different routes. `PathUtils.CalculateCost` applies the same `[0.5,1]` draw outside the search (`PathUtils.cs:34`). This is the mechanism behind "traffic spreads across parallel routes", and a mod that wants reproducible routing sets `Stable`.

`PathfindFlags` in full: `Stable`, `IgnoreFlow`, `ForceForward`, `ForceBackward`, `NoHeuristics`, `ParkingReset`, `Simplified`, `MultipleOrigins`, `MultipleDestinations`, `IgnoreExtraStartAccessRequirements`, `IgnoreExtraEndAccessRequirements`, `IgnorePath`, `SkipPathfind` (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathfindFlags.cs:6-21`).

The result comes back as **`PathInformation { m_Origin, m_Destination, m_Distance, m_Duration, m_TotalCost, m_Methods, m_State }`** on the requesting entity (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathInformation.cs:6-20`) plus a **`PathElement { m_Target, m_TargetDelta, m_Flags }`** buffer, one element per graph edge traversed, `[InternalBufferCapacity(0)]` so it always heap-allocates (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathElement.cs:7-14`). `PathOwner { m_ElementIndex, m_State }` is the cursor into that buffer (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathOwner.cs:6-10`). `PathElementFlags` are `Secondary`, `PathStart`, `Action`, `Return`, `Reverse`, `WaitPosition`, `Leader`, `Hangaround` (`PathElementFlags.cs:6-15`).

`PathFlags` is the 13-member state machine on both `PathOwner` and `PathInformation`: `Pending`, `Failed`, `Obsolete`, `Scheduled`, `Append`, `Updated`, `Stuck`, `WantsEvent`, `AddDestination`, `Debug`, `Divert`, `DivertObsolete`, `CachedObsolete` (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathFlags.cs:6-21`). **`Pending` never survives a save**: both deserializers rewrite it to `Obsolete` (`PathOwner.cs:38-42`, `PathInformation.cs:65-69`), so every in-flight request is re-issued on load rather than resumed. `VehicleUtils.RequireNewPath` is the standard test — `(Obsolete | DivertObsolete)` set and none of `(Pending | Failed | Stuck)` (`src/Game/Game.Vehicles/VehicleUtils.cs:199-206`).

### Congestion feeds back into route choice through one byte

1. **Vehicles record what they did.** `CarNavigationSystem.ApplySideEffects` enqueues, per lane traversed, `m_Flow = float2(duration * maxDriveSpeed, distance) / max(1, curveLength)`, negated for bicycles (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/CarNavigationSystem.cs:2182-2185`). So the first component is *time spent times the speed the vehicle could have done* and the second is *distance actually covered*, both normalised by lane length.
2. **The lane accumulates it.** `ApplyLaneEffectsJob` adds a positive `m_Flow` into `LaneFlow.m_Next` and subtracts a negative one into `SecondaryFlow.m_Next` — bicycles are tracked separately by the sign (`:2697-2710`). The same job adds `sideEffects.x * LaneDeteriorationData.m_TrafficFactor` to `LaneCondition.m_Wear`, **capped at 10** (`:2691-2695`), and `sideEffects.yz` to the owner's `Game.Net.Pollution` (`:2711-2715`).
3. **`TrafficFlowSystem` decays it into a rolling average.** `LaneFlow.m_Duration` and `m_Distance` are `float4`s — **one slot per quarter of the day** — and each is `lerp`ed toward `m_Next` with `t = m_TimeFactors * 0.125f`, then `m_Next` is cleared (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/TrafficFlowSystem.cs:64-84`). `m_TimeFactors` is a saturated four-lobe tent over `TimeSystem.normalizedTime * 4` (`RoadSafetySystem.cs:275-277` builds the identical vector), so a lane's flow history is kept per time-of-day quarter and only the current quarter is written.
4. **Flow becomes a speed penalty.** `NetUtils.GetTrafficFlowSpeed(duration, distance) = saturate(distance / duration)` (`src/Game/Game.Net/NetUtils.cs:760-768`) — a 0-to-1 ratio of achieved to free-flow speed. `UpdateLaneFlow` converts it to `CarLane.m_FlowOffset = clamp(256 - round(dot(flowSpeed, timeFactors) * 256), 0, 255)` and, **only when the byte changes**, enqueues a `FlowActionData` into the pathfinding queue (`TrafficFlowSystem.cs:181-198`).
5. **The pathfinder reads it.** `PathUtils.CalculateSpeed` returns `speed - m_FlowOffset * 0.00390625f * speed` — i.e. free speed reduced by `m_FlowOffset/256` — **unless `PathfindFlags.IgnoreFlow` is set** (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathUtils.cs:54-62`).

**So congestion is a per-lane, per-time-of-day, exponentially-smoothed byte that scales the pathfinder's speed, and the whole feedback runs through `CarLane.m_FlowOffset`.** A mod that wants to change how traffic responds to congestion changes that byte or sets `IgnoreFlow`; a mod that reads congestion reads it.

`TrafficFlowSystem` also rolls lane flow up to the edge. `Road { m_TrafficFlowDuration0, m_TrafficFlowDuration1, m_TrafficFlowDistance0, m_TrafficFlowDistance1, m_Flags }` (`src/Game/Game.Net/Road.cs:7-17`) holds **four `float4`s = two ends × four time-of-day slots**: `UpdateRoadFlowJob` splits each lane's contribution by `EdgeLane.m_EdgeDelta`, sending it to slot 0 when the lane touches delta 0 and slot 1 when it touches delta 1, and **splitting a roundabout lane one-third to each** (`TrafficFlowSystem.cs:155-159`). A lane with no `EdgeLane` and not on a roundabout contributes to **both** slots at weight 1: the no-`EdgeLane` arm is `math.select(1f, 1f/3f, isRoundabout)` applied to both components (`:155`). `TrafficFlowSystem.GetUpdateInterval` returns **512**, and the class declares `public const int UPDATES_PER_DAY = 32` (`:247`, `:273-276`); `cityAverageTrafficFlow` and `cityAverageTrafficVolume` are exposed off the same system (`:269-271`).

`LaneFlow` and `SecondaryFlow` exist on a lane only when its pathfind prefab opts in: `CarLane : ComponentBase.GetArchetypeComponents` adds `LaneFlow` for non-bicycle road types and `SecondaryFlow` for bicycle, **only if the prefab is not a master lane and `NetLanePrefab.m_PathfindPrefab.m_TrackTrafficFlow` is true** (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/CarLane.cs:49-75`), and `NetInitializeSystem` mirrors the flag as `LaneFlags.TrackFlow` on `NetLaneData` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/NetInitializeSystem.cs:1475-1481`). `PathfindPrefab.m_TrackTrafficFlow` defaults to `true` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/PathfindPrefab.cs:8`) — itself a field initializer under the ruling above, though a `bool` rather than a magnitude. Read live, `Car Drive Lane 3` carries `NetLaneData.m_Flags = Road | TrackFlow`.

### Blockage: any stopped vehicle closes a stretch of lane, and the pathfinder routes around it

`LaneDataSystem.CheckBlockage` walks the lane's `LaneObject` buffer and unions the curve-position range of **every object that does not have a `Moving` component** — that is, every stopped one, accident or not (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/LaneDataSystem.cs:727-751`). It reports two extra bits from the same pass: `isEmergency` when any blocking object is accident-involved or carries `CarFlags.Emergency`, and `isSecured` when an accident-involved one has been secured.

`AddBlockageData` writes the union into `CarLane.m_BlockageStart/End` as bytes (`floor(min*255)`, `ceil(max*255)`) and optionally copies it into the caution range (`:753-765`). `PathUtils.GetCarDriveSpecification` then sets `RuleFlags.HasBlockage` and copies the range onto the pathfind edge **whenever `m_BlockageEnd >= m_BlockageStart`** (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathUtils.cs:968-973`), and `PathfindJobs.IsValidDelta` rejects any traversal whose delta range overlaps it (`PathfindJobs.cs:924-926`). An agent may opt out through `m_IgnoredRules & RuleFlags.HasBlockage`.

Note the encoding trap: **an unblocked lane is `m_BlockageStart = 255, m_BlockageEnd = 0`** — start above end, which is what makes the `>=` test mean "blocked" — and that is what `LaneDataSystem` resets to (`:171-172`, `:237-238`) and what the pre-`carLaneBlockage` save deserializer fills in (`src/Game/Game.Net/CarLane.cs:95-96`). A mod reading the raw bytes and assuming `0..255` means "fully blocked" has it exactly backwards.

`Verdict:` the wiki's "Traffic will be blocked on the lanes that are affected by the accident, potentially blocking the road if all lanes are blocked" (https://cs2.paradoxwikis.com/Traffic, outdated banner) is **confirmed and is narrower than the code**: the mechanism keys on *any* non-moving lane object, so a stalled service vehicle, a broken-down car and an accident all block identically. The wiki's separate sentence that agents "might recalculate their pathfinding and make U-turns to find alternative routes circumventing the blocked lane" is confirmed by the `RuleFlags.HasBlockage` path above plus the `Obsolete → RequireNewPath` cycle.

### Accidents: the road-safety formula, whole

`RoadSafetySystem` is the only producer of unprompted traffic accidents, it runs in `SystemUpdatePhase.GameSimulation` (`src/Game/Game.Common/SystemOrder.cs:432`) and `GetUpdateInterval` returns **4096** (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/RoadSafetySystem.cs:240-243`), beside a `private const int UPDATES_PER_DAY = 64` (`:224`). Its query is every non-`Deleted`, non-`Temp` entity with `Edge` + `Composition` and `Any = Road` (`:253-266`).

The job first **discards 63 chunks in 64**: `if (random.NextInt(64) != 0) return;` (`:80-83`). Then, per edge (`:90-131`):

```
duration = dot(m_TrafficFlowDuration0 + m_TrafficFlowDuration1, timeFactors) * 2.6666667
distance = dot(m_TrafficFlowDistance0 + m_TrafficFlowDistance1, timeFactors) * 2.6666667
if (distance < 0.01) skip                                    // no traffic, no accidents
flowSpeed = NetUtils.GetTrafficFlowSpeed(duration, distance) // 0..1

safety  = 500 / sqrt(distance)                               // volume: more traffic, less safe
safety *= lerp(0.5, 1, flowSpeed)                            // congestion: slow flow, less safe
safety *= lerp(1, 0.75, csum(NetCondition.m_Wear) * 0.05)    // wear
safety *= lerp(lit, 1, min(1, brightness * 2))               // darkness
          where lit = 0.9 if RoadFlags.HasStreetLights and not Game.Net.RoadFlags.LightsOff
                    = 0.7 otherwise
safety *= 1.1  if RoadFlags.SeparatedCarriageways
safety  = district StreetTrafficSafety modifier   if not UseHighwayRules
safety  = city HighwayTrafficSafety modifier      if UseHighwayRules
```

and finally `TryStartAccident`, which for each accident prefab whose `TrafficAccidentData.m_RandomSiteType` matches `EventTargetType.Road` and which is not `Locked` rolls `random.NextFloat(1f) < m_OccurenceProbability / max(1f, safety)` and, on a hit, creates the event entity with a `TargetElement` naming the road and returns (`:134-164`).

`m_Brightness` is `LightingSystem.dayLightBrightness` and the time factors are the same four-lobe tent used for flow (`:275-277`, `:296`). The district and city modifiers are `DistrictModifierType.StreetTrafficSafety` applied per side through `BorderDistrict` and reconciled by a min/max rule that keeps the change monotonic (`:112-125`), and `CityModifierType.HighwayTrafficSafety` (`:126-129`).

**Read live, exactly three prefabs carry `TrafficAccidentData`, and only one of them can be selected here**: `Lose Control Accident` (`m_RandomSiteType = Road`, `m_SubjectType = MovingCar`, `m_AccidentType = LoseControl`, `m_OccurenceProbability = 0.1`), `Hail Storm` (`m_RandomSiteType = None`, probability 1) and `Tornado` (`m_RandomSiteType = None`, probability 10). Because the loop tests `m_RandomSiteType == targetType` and `targetType` is `EventTargetType.Road`, **the two disaster prefabs are unreachable from `RoadSafetySystem`**; what does drive them was not traced — the captured read settles only the `None` site type, not the driver. That set is a swept-set shape under the utilities ruling above, and the query that reproduces it is `ecs_query` on `Game.Prefabs.TrafficAccidentData`.

`Verdict:` the wiki says accident likelihood "is calculated per road segment and is increased by features such as road conditions, lighting conditions, weather, and disasters. Keeping roads in good condition… and having streetlights is a good way to decrease accident probability" (https://cs2.paradoxwikis.com/Traffic, outdated banner). **Per-segment, wear and lighting are confirmed** at the lines above. **The wiki is materially incomplete on the two dominant terms**: safety is inversely proportional to the square root of traffic *volume* and is halved again at zero flow speed, so **volume and congestion, which the wiki never mentions, are what actually drive accident rates** — and a road with no traffic on it cannot have an accident at all (`distance < 0.01` returns). The wiki also omits the `SeparatedCarriageways` bonus and both policy modifiers. The decompile owns this and wins.

### Parking: two kinds of lane, and free space carried in the speed field

**A parking lane becomes a zero-length pathfinding edge, and its `m_MaxSpeed` field is repurposed as its free space.** `PathUtils.GetParkingSpaceSpecification` builds `{ m_Flags = Forward|Backward|FreeBackward, m_Length = 0, m_MaxSpeed = max(1, ParkingLane.m_FreeSpace), m_Density = VehicleUtils.GetParkingSize(parkingLaneData).x }` (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathUtils.cs:1187-1196`). This is the single most surprising thing in the topic: **on a parking edge, `m_MaxSpeed` is metres of free kerb (or `m_MaxCarLength`) and `m_Density` is slot width**, and a reader who reads either as what its name says gets nonsense.

The gate is a two-component compare: `PathfindJobs` rejects a direction when `math.any(m_ParkingSize > float2(spec.m_Density, spec.m_MaxSpeed))` (`PathfindJobs.cs:493`, again at `:953`), and `PathfindTargetSeeker` does the same when picking a parking target (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathfindTargetSeeker.cs:507-516`). `m_ParkingSize` is forced to `float.MinValue` — i.e. everything passes — when the request's methods include `PathMethod.Boarding` (`PathfindJobs.cs:258`).

**Free space is computed two entirely different ways** by `ParkingLaneDataSystem.CalculateFreeSpace` (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/ParkingLaneDataSystem.cs:549-733`), and which one runs is decided by `ParkingLaneData.m_SlotInterval != 0f`:

- **Slotted lanes** (parking lots and angled/perpendicular kerbside): walk the curve in 16 steps, step through `NetUtils.GetParkingSlotCount` slots at `NetUtils.GetParkingSlotInterval` spacing, and **return `ParkingLaneData.m_MaxCarLength` the moment one slot is found free** of parked cars, lane overlaps and the blocked range — else 0 (`:555-638`). So a slotted lane reports a *binary* free/full dressed as a length.
- **Continuous kerbside lanes**: walk the sorted parked cars and lane overlaps, measure the gap between consecutive obstacles minus each one's `VehicleUtils.GetParkingOffsets` plus 1, and **return the largest gap**, clamped to `m_MaxCarLength` when that is non-zero (`:639-732`). So a continuous lane reports a real length.

A `ParkingLaneFlags.VirtualLane` returns 0 free space and instead gets `PathMethod.Boarding` only (`:551-554`, `PathUtils.cs:1197-1200`); a `ParkingLaneFlags.ParkingDisabled` lane has its free space forced to 1 (`PathUtils.cs:1218`).

**Fee and comfort enter the cost multiplicatively**, and this is where the money and comfort weights bite hardest:

```
carPathfindData.m_ParkingCost.m_Value.z *= (int)parkingLane.m_ParkingFee
carPathfindData.m_ParkingCost.m_Value.w *= (65535 - parkingLane.m_ComfortFactor) * 1.5259022E-05f
```
(`PathUtils.cs:1232-1233`; `1.5259022E-05f` is 1/65535). So the money term is the prefab's parking money cost **times the lane's fee**, and the comfort term is the prefab's parking comfort cost times **the lane's discomfort**, `m_ComfortFactor` being a `ushort` where 65535 means perfectly comfortable and costs nothing. A garage does the same through `GarageLane.m_ParkingFee`/`m_ComfortFactor` (`PathUtils.cs:1379-1382`), and a garage's `m_MaxSpeed`/`m_Density` are set to `1000000f` when `m_VehicleCount < m_VehicleCapacity` and to `1`/`0` when full (`:1375-1378`) — the same repurposing, used as an on/off capacity switch.

`ParkingLaneData` is `{ m_SlotSize (float2), m_SlotAngle, m_SlotInterval, m_MaxCarLength, m_RoadTypes }` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/ParkingLaneData.cs:8-18`), and its `m_RoadTypes` decides which parking methods the edge offers: non-bicycle → `Parking | Boarding`, bicycle → `BicycleParking`, `ParkingLaneFlags.SpecialVehicles` → `Boarding | SpecialParking` (`PathUtils.cs:1203-1217`).

**Paid parking is a district policy**, not a road property: `LanePoliciesSystem` reads `DistrictOptionData` for `DistrictOption.PaidParking` alongside `ForbidCombustionEngines`, `ForbidTransitTraffic`, `ForbidHeavyTraffic` and `ForbidBicycles` — the whole five-member `DistrictOption` enum (`src/Game/Game.Areas/DistrictOption.cs:3-10`, read at `src/Game/Game.Pathfind/LanePoliciesSystem.cs:288-307`).

### Parking search and re-validation happen every few frames, on the vehicle

A car with a plan does not trust its reserved space. `PersonalCarAISystem.CheckParkingSpace` runs whenever the path is neither pending, failed nor stuck (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/PersonalCarAISystem.cs:399-401`) and:

1. calls `VehicleUtils.ValidateParkingSpace(…, ignoreDriveways: false, ignoreDisabled: true, boardingOnly: false)` and returns if the space is still there (`:410-414`);
2. otherwise picks an index into the remaining path with `random.NextInt(n) * (random.NextInt(n) + 1) / n`, where `n = min(40000, path.Length - m_ElementIndex)` — **a quadratic bias toward the near end of the remaining route**, so a car looks for parking close to where it already is before looking further along (`:415-421`);
3. if the picked element is a parking lane, calls `VehicleUtils.FindFreeParkingSpace` bounded below by the previous element's delta and by the vehicle's own `GetParkingSize(...).y` (`:422-440`);
4. and falls through to re-pathing when that fails.

The `40000` cap and the two-draw bias are C# and ship; the `PathfindFlags.ParkingReset` half-cost above is what makes the re-path cheap.

### Intercity traffic: generated by a spawner, routed with a random tiebreak

**Generation.** `TrafficSpawnerAISystem.Tick` rolls `value = random.NextFloat(rate*0.5, rate*1.5)` where `rate = TrafficSpawnerData.m_SpawnRate * 4.266667f`, and requests a vehicle when `MathUtils.RoundToIntRandom(value) > 0` and no request is already outstanding (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/TrafficSpawnerAISystem.cs:135-143`). The mix is a flat draw over 100: **under 20 → a `DeliveryTruck` at `SizeClass.Large`; 20–24 → a `TransportVehicle` at `Large`; the rest a car** (`:167-180`). A non-car spawner is always a transport vehicle (`:181-185`). Delivery trucks carry `DeliveryTruckFlags.DummyTraffic` and are `Loaded` 75% of the time (`:230-234`).

**On load, the network is pre-populated.** When `m_Loading < 0.9f`, `SpawnVehicle` zeroes the spawn delay, drops the owner, and **places the vehicle at a random point between element 2 and three-quarters along its own path** (`num = random.NextInt(2, bufferData.Length * 3 / 4)`), taking position and rotation from that path element's curve (`:206-222`). Otherwise the vehicle spawns at the connection with `random.NextUInt(256u)` frames of delay (`:201`).

**Routing.** `RandomTrafficDispatchSystem.FindVehicleSource` builds `SetupQueueTarget`s of type `RandomTraffic` and `CurrentLocation`, weights `(1,1,1,1)`, base speed 111.11 m/s, and `m_IgnoredRules = ForbidSlowTraffic | AvoidBicycles` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/RandomTrafficDispatchSystem.cs:217-223`); adds `PathMethod.MediumRoad` for size class ≤ 1, `CargoLoading` for delivery trucks, `ForbidHeavyTraffic` to the ignored rules for size class < 2, and `ForbidCombustionEngines` for electric (`:238-297`); and then **swaps origin and destination on a coin flip** (`:298-300`).

**The candidate pairing is deliberately randomised.** `RoadPathfindSetup.SetupRandomTrafficJob` offers every *other* traffic spawner whose road or track types intersect the request's, each at `cost = random.NextFloat(10000f)` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/RoadPathfindSetup.cs:216-220`). `SetupOutsideConnectionsJob` does the same with `cost = random.NextFloat(m_SetupQueueTarget.m_Value2)` where the caller supplies the range, and 0 when it does not (`:250-259`).

`Verdict:` the wiki says intercity traffic runs "from one outside connection to the next", "does not contribute to the city's economy", and "if the player builds a shorter route between two outside connections, the traffic between those cities will change to use the newly created option if it is more cost-efficient in regard to pathfinding" (https://cs2.paradoxwikis.com/Traffic, outdated banner). **The first two are confirmed** — the origin and destination are both `TrafficSpawner` entities (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/RoadPathfindSetup.cs:216`) and delivery trucks are tagged `DummyTraffic` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/TrafficSpawnerAISystem.cs:230`). **The third is confirmed only as a tendency**: the per-candidate cost is a uniform draw over `[0, 10000)` added before the search, so which pair of connections a given vehicle links is substantially random, and a shorter route shifts the distribution rather than redirecting the traffic. The wiki's flat "will change to use the newly created option" overstates it, and the decompile owns the claim.

### The outside-connection delay is measured congestion fed back as time cost

`Game.Net.OutsideConnection` holds one field, `m_Delay` (`src/Game/Game.Net/OutsideConnection.cs:6-8`); `Game.Objects.OutsideConnection` is a bare tag on the object (`src/Game/Game.Objects/OutsideConnection.cs`). The delay is **measured, not authored**.

`OutsideConnectionDelaySystem` indexes the connection's car connection lanes by start node, resolves the connected edges' road and bicycle sub-lanes through `SlaveLane.m_MasterIndex` to match those start nodes (`:106-124`), and for each matching lane accumulates `CalculateDelay(lane)` — **the summed `CarCurrentLane.m_Duration` of every `LaneObject` whose lane flags include `ResetSpeed`** (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/OutsideConnectionDelaySystem.cs:168-183`). `ResetSpeed` does not mean stopped: it marks a car that just spawned or left a connection lane until it has travelled roughly ten metres, at which point the flag clears and `m_Duration` zeroes (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/CarNavigationSystem.cs:1737-1744`, armed at `:1886-1900` and `TrafficSpawnerAISystem.cs:377`) — so the sum measures **entry dwell**, which grows exactly when the entry is congested. It then writes the **mean** over the contributing lanes into `m_Delay` (`:140`) and enqueues a `TimeActionData` setting the connection edge's time cost to `PathfindConnectionData.m_BorderCost.m_Value.x + m_Delay` (`:146-156`).

`PathUtils.GetSpecification` for a connection lane seeds `m_Costs.m_Value.x = outsideConnection.m_Delay` directly (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathUtils.cs:1349-1355`). **So a queue at a border crossing raises that crossing's time cost for the whole city**, and every agent choosing between two outside connections sees the congestion at each. Read live at 1.6.0f1, `Connection Pathfind` (one entity, the sole carrier of `PathfindConnectionData`) has `m_BorderCost = (60, 0, 10, 60)` and `m_PedestrianBorderCost = (2000, 8000, 0, 2000)` — the pedestrian border cost being enormous by design, which is what stops citizens walking out of the city.

The rest of that component's live values, kept here and not shipped: `m_DistanceCost (0.05,0.05,0.05,0.05)`, `m_AirwayCost (0,0,0.02,0)`, `m_InsideCost (0.01,0.01,0,0)`, `m_AreaCost (0,0.01,0,0)`, `m_CarSpawnCost (5,0,0,0)`, `m_BicycleSpawnCost (5,50,0,100)`, `m_PedestrianSpawnCost (5,0,0,0)`, `m_TaxiStartCost (5,0,50,20)`, `m_ParkingCost (0,0,10,200)`, `m_BicycleParkingCost (0,0,10,50)`.

### The pathfinding queue: three queues, two graph copies, half the worker threads

`PathfindQueueSystem` is registered with `UpdateBefore` in `SystemUpdatePhase.MainLoop` (`src/Game/Game.Common/SystemOrder.cs:48`), with `PathfindSetupSystem` and `PathfindResultSystem` `UpdateAt` the same phase (`:64-65`).

**Nine action types, three priority classes.** `ActionType` is `Create, Update, Delete, Pathfind, Coverage, Availability, Density, Time, Flow` (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathfindQueueSystem.cs:82-93`), each with its own `ActionList<T>` (`:275-291`). Enqueueing routes into one of three `Queue<ActionType>`s: `m_HighPriorityTypes` (an item inserted at `m_NextIndex + m_PriorityCount++`, jumping the line), `m_ModificationTypes` (Create/Update/Delete/Density/Time/Flow), and `m_ActionTypes` (everything else) (`:580-613`). `OnUpdate` drains them strictly in that order — high priority, then modifications, then normal — and **returns early the moment the next item's job dependencies are not yet complete** (`:758-956`).

**The graph is double-buffered and modifications write both copies.** `WORKER_DATA_COUNT = 2` (`:267`), and every modification action goes through `ScheduleModificationJob`, which schedules the job **on both `WorkerData`s**, combining each one's outstanding write *and read* handles as its dependency, and clearing the read handle (`:615-631`). Reads go to `m_WorkerData[m_NextWorkerIndex]` (`:427-432`), and the index advances only when the last reader and the next writer are the same buffer (`:626-629`).

**This is the cost structure a mod has to respect.** A pathfind, coverage or availability action is *added to a worker action list* and run by worker jobs; a Create/Update/Delete/Density/Time/Flow action **first flushes any pending worker jobs (`ScheduleWorkerJobs`) and then takes a write lock on both graph copies**. So **every network edit serialises the entire pathfinder**, and a mod that edits the network every frame stalls every query in the game every frame.

**Thread budget.** `m_MaxThreadCount = math.max(1, JobsUtility.JobWorkerCount / 2)` (`:326`) — the pathfinder deliberately takes **half** the job worker threads. `ScheduleWorkerJobs` launches `min(actionCount, max(m_MaxThreadCount, highPriorityCount))` copies of `PathfindWorkerJob` (`:640`), each pulling actions off a shared `Interlocked.Increment` cursor until the list is exhausted (`:215-243`). Each worker thread gets a persistent `UnsafeLinearAllocator` initialised at **1 MiB** (`1048576u`, `:673`), rewound after every action and resized at the end of the job (`:240-242`).

`GetGraphSize()`, `GetGraphMemory(out used, out allocated)` and `GetQueryMemory(out used, out allocated)` are public and are the supported way to see what the graph costs (`:445-478`).

### What running inside the queue costs: the simulation clock is throttled by the pathfinder

Every queue a system takes carries a deadline. `PathfindSetupSystem.GetQueue(object system, int maxDelayFrames, int spreadFrames = 0)` sets `m_ResultFrame = frameIndex + maxDelayFrames` and `m_SpreadFrame = frameIndex + spreadFrames`, and folds the result frame into `m_QueueSimulationFrameIndex` with a `min` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/PathfindSetupSystem.cs:395-420`). `pendingSimulationFrame` on both the setup and result systems is the `min` over everything outstanding (`:207`, `src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathfindResultSystem.cs:123`).

`SimulationSystem.OnUpdate` then reads it (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/SimulationSystem.cs:231-245`):

```
slack = max(0, pendingSimulationFrame - frameIndex - 1)
dt   *= min(1, slack / 48)                     // the frame's time step is scaled down
…
frames = min(frames, slack)                    // and the frame count is hard-capped
```

**So when the pathfinding queue falls behind its own deadline, the simulation slows down: the game literally cannot outrun the pathfinder.** Inside 48 frames of the nearest deadline the time step is scaled linearly toward zero, and at the deadline the simulation advances no frames at all. `DebugSystem` displays the same quantity (`src/Game/Game.Debug/DebugSystem.cs:7064-7066`). A mod that enqueues pathfind requests with a short `maxDelayFrames`, or that enqueues many, is not just spending CPU — it is putting a brake on the game clock.

**Ruled (2026-08-14, the roads-and-traffic pass, made by the orchestrating session under the maintainer's delegated authority for that pass; conflicts.md).** The throttle ships plainly, as mechanism. The reference states the formula whole — the running minimum over outstanding deadlines, the 48-frame linear ramp on the time step, the frame cap — cited to its lines, and states the consequence as behaviour in the topic's own voice: when the queue falls behind its deadlines the simulation slows, and at a deadline it advances no frames. No shipped sentence characterises the design or instructs restraint — no *must not*, no *badly degrades* — the mechanism and the vanilla convention carry the warning, as the city-services ruling shipped surprising behaviour flat with the surprise leading. The census of deadlines ships as its shape per ADR 0006 — reactive at 64 frames, population-wide at 80 spread over 16, background at 512 — with the sweep that produced it named as the derivation rather than as a roster, and at most a cited worked example per class. The mechanism stays whole in this topic; `performance-and-memory` is touched only if it also states what throttles the simulation clock, and a bridge between the two carries no part of it.

**The vanilla census of deadlines is short enough to ship as a shape.** Across the whole decompile there are **46** `GetQueue(this, …)` call sites: **40 at `(this, 64)`**, **4 at `(this, 80, 16)`**, **1 at `(this, 512)`**, and **1 passing a variable** — `RandomTrafficDispatchSystem.cs:442`, whose `maxDelayFrames` is 16 while `SimulationSystem.loadingProgress` is below 1 — a state only the new-game warm-up holds, since `m_LoadingCount` is nonzero only for `Purpose.NewGame` (`SimulationSystem.cs:160`) — and 64 after (`:412-423`), so it is a reactive site with a warm-up tightening rather than a fourth class. The four spread queues are exactly the four bulk citizen-demand systems — `FindJobSystem.cs:583`, `HouseholdFindPropertySystem.cs:1179`, `ResourceBuyerSystem.cs:1309`, `TripNeededSystem.cs:2151` — and the single long-deadline queue is `AreaLotSimulationSystem.cs:1166`. So the convention is: **64 frames for anything reactive, 80 frames spread over 16 for anything that asks on behalf of a whole population, 512 for background work.** A mod adding a queue should match the class of work it is doing.

**`spreadFrames` is the throttle, and it is per-frame proportional.** When `m_SpreadFrame > frameIndex`, the setup system dequeues only `ceil(queueCount * (step / (remainingFrames + step)))` items this frame, where `step` is `Time.deltaTime * smoothSpeed * 60` or 1 when paused (`PathfindSetupSystem.cs:310-317`). That is what keeps a population-wide re-plan from arriving as one spike.

**Setup itself is a two-stage sort.** `OnUpdate` builds a `SetupListItem` per origin and per destination, **sorts the whole list by `SetupTargetType`** (`CompareTo` is `m_Target.m_Type - other.m_Target.m_Type`, `:83-86`, sorted at `:346`), then runs one `FindTargets` job per contiguous run of the same type (`:350-363`). `CompleteSetup` gathers each item's buffer back onto its action and enqueues the lot (`:366-393`).

**`SetupTargetType` is a closed enum — 47 target types plus `None = -1` (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/SetupTargetType.cs:3-53`) — dispatched by a hardcoded `switch`.** `PathfindSetupSystem.FindTargets` maps each member to one of twelve `*PathfindSetup` helper classes, and the `default` branch is `Debug.LogWarning("Invalid target type in Pathfind setup " + targetType)` returning an empty job handle (`:454-556`). `SetupTargetType.Sightseeing` already takes that shape deliberately, returning `default(JobHandle)` at `:544-545`. **A mod therefore cannot add a target type**: a new enum value falls to the warning branch and silently produces no targets. A mod's route into this system is `GetQueue` plus an existing `SetupTargetType`, most usefully `CurrentLocation` (`SetupTargetType.cs:6`), which is the generic "where this entity is" case.

`SetupQueueItem { m_Owner, m_Parameters, m_Origin, m_Destination }` (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/SetupQueueItem.cs:5-13`) and `SetupQueueTarget` with its sixteen fields (`SetupQueueTarget.cs:9-40`) are the request shape; `SetupTargetFlags` is `Industrial, Commercial, Import, Service, Residential, Export, SecondaryPath, RequireTransport, PathEnd, BuildingUpkeep` (`SetupTargetFlags.cs:6-19`).

### The queue's other five action types, and the boundary they sit on

`Coverage` and `Availability` are the other two *query* types run by the same worker jobs (`PathfindQueueSystem.cs:230-264`), producing `CoverageElement` and `AvailabilityElement` buffers through `CoverageJobs` and `AvailabilityJobs`. Their cost functions are not the four-weight dot product: coverage is `length * density * |Δ|` (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathUtils.cs:37-40`) and availability is `length * lerp(1, density, m_DensityWeight) * |Δ| * m_CostFactor / m_MaxSpeed` (`:42-46`). **They belong to `city-services-and-coverage`, not here** — but they share the queue, so a mod that saturates the pathfinder degrades service coverage too, and vice versa.

`Density`, `Time` and `Flow` are the three *modification* types that write scalar fields onto existing edges without rebuilding them — `ModificationJobs.SetDensityJob`, `SetTimeJob`, `SetFlowJob` (`PathfindQueueSystem.cs:892-942`). `Flow` is the one this topic uses, enqueued by `TrafficFlowSystem` when `m_FlowOffset` changes; `Time` is enqueued by `OutsideConnectionDelaySystem`.

### How a mod makes the network re-derive: three triggers of very different weight

The whole net-modification pipeline runs inside the `Modification*` phases, in a fixed order that a mod ordering itself against must respect (`src/Game/Game.Common/SystemOrder.cs`):

- **Modification2B**: `Game.Net.ReferencesSystem` (`:125`), `NodeReductionSystem` (`:128`), `NodeAlignSystem` (`:129`), `AggregateSystem` (`:130`).
- **Modification3**: `CompositionSelectSystem` (`:139`) — where an edge's `CompositionFlags` become composition prefab references.
- **Modification4**: `NetCompositionSystem` (`:148`), `Game.Net.GeometrySystem` (`:150`), `NetComponentsSystem` (`:151`), `OutsideConnectionSystem` (`:154`), **`LaneSystem` (`:155`)**, `AddAccidentSiteSystem` (`:167`).
- **Modification4B**: `LaneReferencesSystem` (`:175`), `LaneOverlapSystem` (`:178`), `RoadConnectionSystem` (`:180`), `ParkedVehiclesSystem` (`:181`), `TrafficLightInitializationSystem` (`:182`), `SecondaryLaneSystem` (`:184`).
- **Modification5**: `Game.Net.UpdateCollectSystem` (`:192`), `Game.Net.SearchSystem` (`:198`), `LaneConnectionSystem` (`:211`), `LaneBlockSystem` (`:218`), `Game.Net.OverrideSystem` (`:220`), `FixLaneObjectsSystem` (`:221`), `Game.Net.InitializeSystem` (`:224`), `EdgeMappingSystem` (`:225`), `LanePoliciesSystem` (`:238`), `LaneDataUnknownEscalateSystem` (`:239`).
- **ModificationEnd**: `LaneDataSystem` (`:249`), `RouteDataSystem` (`:250`), `ParkingLaneDataSystem` (`:251`), **`LanesModifiedSystem` (`:252`)**, `RoutesModifiedSystem` (`:253`), `PathOwnerTargetMovedSystem` (`:257`).

Three triggers, cheapest first:

1. **`Game.Common.PathfindUpdated`** — a zero-size tag (`src/Game/Game.Common/PathfindUpdated.cs:7-9`) picked up by `LaneDataSystem`'s second query alongside `Lane` and any lane payload, excluding `Updated`, `Deleted` and `Temp` (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/LaneDataSystem.cs:1026-1044`). It **re-derives a lane's pathfind specification without relaning anything**. The game uses it for exactly this: a parked car appearing or leaving (`src/Game/Game.Events/ImpactSystem.cs:268`, `:293`, `:320`), a lane connection changing (`src/Game/Game.Net/LaneConnectionSystem.cs:414`/`:427`/`:436`), a district boundary moving (`src/Game/Game.Areas/CurrentDistrictSystem.cs:256`), a district policy changing (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/LanePoliciesSystem.cs:81`/`:85`). It is **a one-frame tag**: `CleanUpSystem` removes it (`src/Game/Game.Common/CleanUpSystem.cs:31`, with `PrepareCleanUpSystem.cs:38`).
2. **`Updated`** on a lane — same `LaneDataSystem` query, first branch (`LaneDataSystem.cs:1005-1023`), and also what `LanesModifiedSystem`'s updated query wants (`LanesModifiedSystem.cs:1064-1085`), producing an `UpdateAction` on the pathfinding queue.
3. **`Updated` on the edge or node** — `LaneSystem`'s query is `All = SubLane`, `Any = { Updated, Deleted }`, `None = { OutsideConnection, Game.Objects.OutsideConnection, Area }` (`src/Game/Game.Net/LaneSystem.cs:9180-9194`). **This is the full relane**, and `LaneSystem` is the largest file in `Game.Net` at over 9,300 lines. It runs in Modification4, so everything from `LaneReferencesSystem` onwards re-runs behind it in the same frame.

`LanesModifiedSystem` is the bridge into the graph: created lanes → `CreateAction`, updated → `UpdateAction`, deleted → `DeleteAction`, with **`SlaveLane` excluded from the car/parking/pedestrian/connection queries and `TrackLane` handled by a second query with no such exclusion** (`LanesModifiedSystem.cs:1035-1085`). The specification each one carries is built by the `PathUtils.Get*Specification` family (`:163`, `:207`, `:243`, `:277`, `:318`, `:367`).

**Making one *agent* re-path is a different act from making the *network* re-derive, and it has its own two levers.** Vanilla's is `PathFlags.Obsolete` on the `PathOwner`, which `VehicleUtils.SetTarget` sets while clearing `Failed` (`src/Game/Game.Vehicles/VehicleUtils.cs:213-218`) and which `RequireNewPath` then acts on (`:199-206`). The corpus shows a second: `Time2Work` forces a repath by **removing the `PathInformation` component and the `PathElement` buffer outright** rather than by setting any flag (`Time2Work/NightShift/Systems/SocialTripSystem.cs:373-377`). Neither touches the network, and a mod that adds `Updated` to an edge when it only wanted one citizen to reconsider has relaned the whole segment for nothing.

`Rots:` this whole ordering. `SystemOrder.cs` is regenerated per version and the line numbers move on every build; re-check `src/Game/Game.Common/SystemOrder.cs` by system name rather than by line.

### Deterioration, condition and maintenance

`NetCondition { m_Wear }` is a `float2` on the edge (`src/Game/Game.Net/NetCondition.cs`), `LaneCondition { m_Wear }` a `float` on the lane (`src/Game/Game.Net/LaneCondition.cs`). Wear accrues from traffic in `CarNavigationSystem`'s lane-effects job as `m_Wear = min(m_Wear + sideEffects.x * LaneDeteriorationData.m_TrafficFactor, 10f)` — **capped at 10, a C# literal** (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/CarNavigationSystem.cs:2693`). `LaneDeteriorationData { m_TrafficFactor, m_TimeFactor }` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/LaneDeteriorationData.cs:5-9`) comes from `LaneDeterioration : ComponentBase`, whose `GetArchetypeComponents` adds `LaneCondition` **only when the lane is not a master lane** (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/LaneDeterioration.cs:20-26`). Read live at 1.6.0f1, thirteen lane prefabs carry `LaneDeteriorationData`, and the three sampled — `Car Drive Lane 3`, `Gravel Drive Lane 3`, `Alley Drive Lane 3` — all carry `(0.01, 0.5)`, matching the class initializers exactly.

Wear feeds accidents through `lerp(1, 0.75, csum(m_Wear) * 0.05)` in the safety formula above, and `RoadPrefab` puts `MaintenanceConsumer` on every road edge archetype (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/RoadPrefab.cs:63`). The maintenance service itself is `city-services-and-coverage`'s.

`ApplySideEffects` also drives noise and air pollution: `sideEffects` is `lerp(VehicleSideEffectData.m_Min, m_Max, saturate((achievedSpeed/maxSpeed)²))` scaled by `float3(min(1, distance/laneLength), duration, duration)`, and its `yz` go into the owning edge's `Game.Net.Pollution` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/CarNavigationSystem.cs:2172-2185`, `:2711-2715`). **That is `environment-and-pollution`'s to own**; what belongs here is that the emission rate is quadratic in the achieved-over-maximum speed ratio, lerped between the vehicle prefab's `VehicleSideEffectData.m_Min` and `m_Max`, and the emitted `yz` scale by `m_Duration` — time on the lane — so the per-vehicle comparison between a free-flowing and a congested road is a product of an increased rate and a decreased dwell, not settled by the rate alone.

### Traffic lights are demand-actuated, not fixed-cycle

`TrafficLights { m_State, m_Flags, m_SignalGroupCount, m_CurrentSignalGroup, m_NextSignalGroup, m_Timer }` sits on a node (`src/Game/Game.Net/TrafficLights.cs`), with `TrafficLightState` = `None, Beginning, Ongoing, Ending, Changing, Extending, Extended` (`src/Game/Game.Net/TrafficLightState.cs:3-12`) and `TrafficLightFlags` = `LevelCrossing, MoveableBridge, IsSubNode` (`TrafficLightFlags.cs:6-11`).

Each controlled lane carries `LaneSignal { m_Petitioner, m_Blocker, m_GroupMask, m_Priority, m_Default, m_Signal, m_Flags }` (`src/Game/Game.Net/LaneSignal.cs`), where `m_Signal` is `None, Stop, SafeStop, Yield, Go` (`LaneSignalType.cs:3-11`) and `m_Flags` is `CanExtend, Physical` (`LaneSignalFlags.cs:3-7`).

`TrafficLightSystem.GetNextSignalGroup` picks the next phase by **scanning every lane signal for the highest `m_Priority`, clamped to 127 normally and to 1 on a moveable bridge**, accumulating the group masks of every signal at that priority, tracking which of them may extend, and resetting each signal's priority to `m_Default` as it goes (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/TrafficLightSystem.cs:361-397`). `RequireEnding` returns true when any lane still showing `Go` is not in the next group (`:343-358`). So **phase order and length follow the petitioning traffic**, and `m_Petitioner` is the entity that asked.

`GetUpdateInterval` returns **4** (`:731-734`), and the query is filtered by `SimulationUtils.GetUpdateFrameWithInterval(frameIndex, GetUpdateInterval(…), 16)`, so **the junctions are spread over 16 update-frame groups and each is touched every 64 frames** (`:750`). The class also carries `private const uint UPDATE_INTERVAL = 64u` (`:721`), which the filter never reads — the same unused-constant shape as `RoadSafetySystem`'s `UPDATES_PER_DAY`. `TrafficLightInitializationSystem` builds the groups in Modification4B (`src/Game/Game.Common/SystemOrder.cs:182`).

### Bottlenecks, blockers and the deadlock flag

`Blocker { m_Blocker, m_Type, m_MaxSpeed }` on a vehicle names what is in front of it (`src/Game/Game.Vehicles/Blocker.cs:7-13`), with `BlockerType` declaring `None, Continuing, Crossing, Signal, Temporary, Limit, Caution, Spawn, Oncoming` (`src/Game/Game.Vehicles/BlockerType.cs:3-14`); `src/Game/Game.Simulation/StuckMovingObjectSystem.cs:82`/`:86` and `TrafficBottleneckSystem.cs:73` are the consumer sites this section leans on.

**`StuckMovingObjectSystem` is the deadlock detector.** It runs every 4 frames (`GetUpdateInterval` returns 4, `StuckMovingObjectSystem.cs:322-325`), skips blockers that are null or `Temporary`, and only considers a vehicle blocked when `blocker.m_MaxSpeed < 6` (`:99-102`). `IsBlocked` then **walks the blocker chain up to 100 hops**, returning true when the chain reaches 100 or **cycles back to the starting entity**, and false as soon as it finds a null blocker, a `Temporary` one, or one moving at `m_MaxSpeed >= 6` (`:171-205`). A vehicle that fails the test gets `PathFlags.Stuck` on its `PathOwner`, or `CreatureLaneFlags.Stuck` on its `AnimalCurrentLane` (`:153-167`). It also sets `CarLaneFlags.RequestSpace` on whatever is blocking a `Crossing` (`:86-98`).

**`Stuck` is a terminal state for the current plan, not a despawn.** `VehicleUtils.RequireNewPath` explicitly refuses to re-path a stuck owner (`src/Game/Game.Vehicles/VehicleUtils.cs:199-206`), and each AI reacts differently: a service vehicle treats `IsStuck` the same as `Returning` and turns for base (`AmbulanceAISystem.cs:234`, `FireEngineAISystem.cs:383`, `GarbageTruckAISystem.cs:263`, `HearseAISystem.cs:230`, `MaintenanceVehicleAISystem.cs:291`, `PoliceCarAISystem.cs:407`, `PostVanAISystem.cs:363`, `DeliveryTruckAISystem.cs:732`/`:747`), while a personal car whose path ends without a passenger to disembark is deleted outright by `VehicleUtils.DeleteVehicle` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/PersonalCarAISystem.cs:353-357`, `:382`). `ResidentAISystem` sets `Stuck` itself when a resident cannot find a lane to emerge on after a pathfind failure, and flags the creature `EmergeUnspawned` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/ResidentAISystem.cs:1657-1662`).

`Unconfirmed:` the full despawn rule for a stuck personal car carrying passengers. The three sites above establish deletion for the empty case and disembark-then-park for the transporting case, but the interaction between `Stuck`, `PersonalCarFlags.Disembarking` and `DeleteVehicle` was not traced end to end. Settling it is a live breakpoint on `VehicleUtils.DeleteVehicle` with a condition on the vehicle entity; not run.

**`TrafficBottleneckSystem`** is the player-facing half. It forms connected groups over `Blocker` chains of type `Continuing` (`TrafficBottleneckSystem.cs:65-91`), adds `Game.Net.Bottleneck(minPos, maxPos, 5)` to the lane at the head of a group (`:300`) — the `5` being a countdown timer, and `Bottleneck.m_Position` a midpoint computed as `(minPos + maxPos + 1) >> 1` (`src/Game/Game.Net/Bottleneck.cs`) — removes stale ones (`:334`, `:342`, `:354`, `:362`), and enqueues `new TriggerAction(TriggerType.TrafficBottleneck, Entity.Null, count)` with the total (`:105`). It logs `"TrafficBottleneckSystem: Self blocking entity {index}"` when a vehicle blocks itself (`:84`), which is a real diagnostic string to grep the player log for. The notification prefabs it raises come from `TrafficConfigurationData`, which is nine notification prefab entities and nothing else — `m_BottleneckNotification`, `m_DeadEndNotification`, `m_RoadConnectionNotification`, `m_TrackConnectionNotification`, `m_CarConnectionNotification`, `m_ShipConnectionNotification`, `m_TrainConnectionNotification`, `m_PedestrianConnectionNotification`, `m_BicycleConnectionNotification` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/TrafficConfigurationData.cs:5-24`).

### Roundabout lane counts are per-arc, not per-roundabout

`Verdict:` the wiki's `Roads` page says "The number of lanes on the roundabout depends on the number of lanes in the roads connecting to it" (https://cs2.paradoxwikis.com/Roads, Roundabouts section flagged outdated, last verified 1.1). **Directionally confirmed, and the real rule is finer.** `LaneSystem.GetRoundaboutLaneCount` computes a count for **each arc between two consecutive arms**, not one for the whole roundabout (`src/Game/Game.Net/LaneSystem.cs:1182-1229`):

- `num2` is the arc's angular span quantised to quarter-turns: `max(1, ceil(angle * 2/π - 0.003141593f))`, with a full `2π` when the two arms coincide (`:1186-1187`).
- `num4` counts source positions on the previous arm matching its road types, `num5` counts target positions on the next arm matching its road types (`:1200-1215`).
- For a single-quadrant arc, the count is `max(1, num4)` reduced by at most 1 when both source and target lanes exist, the reduction gated on `GetRoundaboutTargetLaneCount` — the number of non-U-turn, non-bicycle-only road targets available from the last source (`:1217-1226`, `:1231-1244`).
- For a wider arc it is `max(1, max(1,num4) - (num5 - (num5 <= 1 ? 1 : 0)))` (`:1227-1228`).

So the arc carries **the entering arm's lane count minus what leaves at the next arm, floored at 1**, and handedness enters through `m_LeftHandTraffic` choosing `RotationAngleRight` or `RotationAngleLeft` (`:1186`). `Game.Net.Roundabout { m_Radius }` is the marker component on the node (`src/Game/Game.Net/Roundabout.cs`), and `GeometryFlags.SupportRoundabout` on the road's `NetGeometryData` is what allows one at all (`src/Game/Game.Net/GeometryFlags.cs`, read live on both `Small Road` and `Highway Twoway - 3 lanes`).

### Lane overlaps carry the give-way data at conflict points

`LaneOverlap` is a sorted buffer on a lane: `{ m_Other, m_Flags, m_ThisStart, m_ThisEnd, m_OtherStart, m_OtherEnd, m_Parallelism, m_PriorityDelta }`, where the four range bytes are curve positions × 255, `m_Parallelism` is × 128, and `m_PriorityDelta` is an `sbyte` (`src/Game/Game.Net/LaneOverlap.cs`). `OverlapFlags` is `MergeStart, MergeEnd, OverlapLeft, OverlapRight, MergeMiddleStart, MergeMiddleEnd, Unsafe, Road, Track, MergeFlip, Slow, Water` (`src/Game/Game.Net/OverlapFlags.cs:4-18`). `LaneOverlapSystem` builds them in Modification4B (`src/Game/Game.Common/SystemOrder.cs:178`), and `ParkingLaneDataSystem.CalculateFreeSpace` reads the same buffer to exclude overlapped stretches from parking (`ParkingLaneDataSystem.cs:582-586`, `:655-661`).

`LaneReservation { m_Blocker, m_Next, m_Prev }` with `GetOffset()` returning `max(next,prev).m_Offset * 0.003921569f` and `GetPriority()` returning `max(next,prev).m_Priority` (`src/Game/Game.Net/LaneReservation.cs`) is the runtime claim a vehicle stakes on a conflict point; `NetLaneReservationSystem` runs it in `GameSimulation` (`SystemOrder.cs:357`).

### District policies reach individual lanes

`LanePoliciesSystem` (Modification5, `SystemOrder.cs:238`) translates a district's `DistrictOptionData` into lane state, reading the five-member `DistrictOption` enum — `PaidParking`, `ForbidCombustionEngines`, `ForbidTransitTraffic`, `ForbidHeavyTraffic`, `ForbidBicycles` (`src/Game/Game.Areas/DistrictOption.cs:3-10`, at `LanePoliciesSystem.cs:288-307`) — and tagging affected sub-lanes `PathfindUpdated` (`:81`, `:85`). Four of the five map one-to-one onto `RuleFlags` members, which is why the pathfinder's behaviour term is the mechanism that enforces district policy.

`LaneDataSystem` applies the speed half: it clamps `CarLane.m_SpeedLimit` toward a district-derived bound with a monotonic min/max rule (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/LaneDataSystem.cs:703-711`) and sets `CarLaneFlags.ForbidBicycles` from either `DistrictOption.ForbidBicycles` or the composition side's `CompositionFlags.Side.ForbidSecondary` (`:698-724`). `LaneDataUnknownEscalateSystem` (Modification5, `SystemOrder.cs:239`) re-tags sub-lanes `PathfindUpdated` when a lane's policy state could not be resolved on the first pass (`LaneDataUnknownEscalateSystem.cs:70`, `:82`, `:100`, `:104`).

### What the corpus shows: taking over the lane system from the inside

The corpus settles nothing about the game. What it gives here, and nothing else does, is which vanilla systems and components an author had to disable, fork or re-derive to change this behaviour — the network model read from the inside by someone who ran it.

**`Traffic` (krzychu124/Traffic, at `43f5ee1`) disables `Game.Net.LaneSystem` and substitutes a fork in the same ordering slot.** The whole registration is two lines: `Traffic/Code/Mod.cs:76` sets `World.GetOrCreateSystemManaged<LaneSystem>().Enabled = false`, and `:77` registers `UpdateBefore<TrafficLaneSystem, LaneSystem>(SystemUpdatePhase.Modification4)` — anchoring **before the disabled system** rather than at the phase, which is what puts the fork in the original's position instead of at the phase's tail. Vanilla registers `LaneSystem` at exactly that phase (`src/Game/Game.Common/SystemOrder.cs:155`, verified here).

**Substitution is not optional for this system, and the reason is a first-party API that cannot serve two callers.** `Game.Net.LaneReferencesSystem.GetSkipLaneQueue()` **allocates a fresh `NativeQueue<Lane>(Allocator.TempJob)` and overwrites the field on every call** (`src/Game/Game.Net/LaneReferencesSystem.cs:753-757`, read here), and `AddSkipLaneWriter` **overwrites** `m_SkipLaneDeps` rather than combining it (`:759-762`). So a fork that left vanilla enabled would have one of the two queues orphaned and one of the two dependencies lost. The fork therefore re-registers on both sides of the plumbing its original served — `m_TerrainSystem.AddCPUHeightReader`, `m_LaneReferencesSystem.AddSkipLaneWriter`, `m_ModificationBarrier.AddJobHandleForProducer` (`Traffic/Code/Systems/Traffic_LaneSystem.cs:456-458`), matching vanilla at `src/Game/Game.Net/LaneSystem.cs:9303-9304`.

**The fork's form is itself the finding.** `Traffic_LaneSystem.cs` is 9,659 lines against vanilla's 9,326, with the mod's own changes flagged throughout by `NON-STOCK` markers in three forms — paired begin/end blocks, bare inline `/*NON-STOCK*/` tags on added parameters and call-site arguments, and a `/// NON-STOCK` doc line on an added method — plus `/*NON VANILLA - START*/`…`/*NON VANILLA - END*/` fencing the fork's extra job inputs — sampled at `:104-112`, `:1038-1042`, `:1241-1326`, `:1613`, `:6866-6872`, `:7622-7631`, `:7683-7685`, with the fork's extra job inputs fenced as one block at `:713-724`. Its query is vanilla's character for character (`:313-334` against `src/Game/Game.Net/LaneSystem.cs:9180-9194`). And because the source generator no longer writes the type-handle struct for it, **it refreshes every handle by hand through `SystemAPI.Get*` at the top of each update** (`:347-448`) — the concrete answer to what a forked system owes once it leaves the generator behind.

**Five network-model facts the mod had to learn, each of which the decompile confirms:**

1. **A master lane is keyed by lane *group*, not lane index, and suppressing a sub-lane orphans its master.** The mod re-keys its own connections by group (`Traffic_LaneSystem.cs:105-111`) and skips any master whose group produced no custom connection (`:1369-1380`). This is the same master/slave split established above from `MasterLane`/`SlaveLane` and from `LanesModifiedSystem`'s `None = { SlaveLane }`.
2. **A lane's identity at an edge end is a lane index packed into a `PathNode`, and it is composition-relative.** The mod extracts it as `lane.m_EndNode.GetLaneIndex() & 0xFF` (`Traffic/Code/Systems/LaneConnections/GenerateConnectorsSystem.GenerateConnectorsJob.cs:236-243`), against `PathNode.GetLaneIndex` at `src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathNode.cs:118-121`. When the road's composition changes the index moves, so the mod re-maps old to new by matching the lane's cross-section position against `NetCompositionData.m_MiddleOffset` plus its Road/Track flags (`SyncCustomLaneConnectionsSystem.SyncConnectionsJob.cs:481-533`). **It paid for that lesson**: its persisted element gained `carriagewayAndGroup` and `lanePosition` beside the index (`Traffic/Code/Components/LaneConnections/ModifiedLaneConnections.cs:11-15`), and the migration constant that introduced them is commented *"[mod pre-v.1.7.3] incomplete composition information lead to corrupted data after update"* (`Traffic/Code/DataMigrationVersion.cs:5`).
3. **Which end of an edge is "start" depends on `CompositionFlags.General.Invert` and on left-hand traffic**, and the two invert each other (`SyncConnectionsJob.cs:413-416`).
4. **Which composition changes matter is a hand-picked mask**: `ForbidLeftTurn | ForbidStraight | ForbidRightTurn | PrimaryTrack | SecondaryTrack | TertiaryTrack | QuaternaryTrack | PrimaryLane | SecondaryLane` (`SyncConnectionsJob.cs:426-427`) — every member of it in the `directionalMask` set at `src/Game/Game.Prefabs/CompositionFlags.cs:102`. A roundabout node or a changed road prefab invalidates unconditionally (`:457-462`, `:475-477`).
5. **Vanilla turn restrictions live on `Upgraded` and fight custom connections**, so the tool strips `ForbidStraight | ForbidLeftTurn | ForbidRightTurn` from the correct side of `Upgraded.m_Flags` and removes the component when the flags go empty (`Traffic/Code/Tools/LaneConnectorToolSystem.SelectIntersectionNodeJob.cs:62-80`, against `src/Game/Game.Net/Upgraded.cs`).

**The connector-eligibility walk is a compact statement of the lane taxonomy** and matches what this file derived independently: skip any sub-lane without `EdgeLane` or carrying `SecondaryLane`; skip unless `EdgeLane.m_EdgeDelta` matches the end; tag master/slave from component presence; **skip `Utility | Pedestrian | Parking | ParkingLeft | ParkingRight` outright** (`GenerateConnectorsSystem.GenerateConnectorsJob.cs:121-196`), walking with the vanilla `Game.Net.EdgeIterator` (`:73`).

**The rebuild dance is the practical answer to "how does a mod make the network re-derive".** `Traffic` has a named helper: `Updated` on the node, on **every connected edge**, and on **each of those edges' far node** (`Traffic/Code/Systems/Serialization/TrafficDataMigrationSystem.ValidateLoadedDataJob.cs:398-416`), repeated at every site that mutates its data (`LaneConnectorToolSystem.ApplyLaneConnectionsActionJob.cs:269-289`, `TLEDataMigrationSystem.MigrateCustomLaneDirectionsJob.cs:174-185`, and others). **The far node matters because lane generation at a node depends on the composition of edges whose other end may have changed.** It adds no `BatchesUpdated` and no `Created` — it never touches rendering.

**There is no yield component: a priority sign is a flag written onto a generated lane, which is why the feature needs the whole lane system.** `Traffic` writes it inside the fork at lane-creation time (`Traffic/Code/Systems/Traffic_LaneSystem.cs:4792-4815`):

```csharp
component5.m_Flags |= priority == PriorityType.Stop ? CarLaneFlags.Stop :
priority == PriorityType.Yield ? CarLaneFlags.Yield :
priority == PriorityType.RightOfWay ? CarLaneFlags.RightOfWay : 0;
```

Those are the same three `CarLaneFlags` members `LaneSystem.CreateNodeCarLanes` sets from `CalculateYieldOffset`'s 1/2/−1 (`src/Game/Game.Net/LaneSystem.cs:7114-7128`), and the lane is regenerated from scratch on every relane — so there is nowhere to write a sign *after* generation, and no component to add. That is the whole reason this feature costs a nine-thousand-line fork.

## Bridge

**Changing a road's balance means changing prefab data, not code.** Speed limits, pathfind costs, accident probability and deterioration all live on components written during prefab initialisation — `RoadData` from `RoadPrefab` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/NetInitializeSystem.cs:1944-1960`), `PathfindCarData` from `CarPathfind.Initialize` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/CarPathfind.cs:37-52`), `TrafficAccidentData` from `TrafficAccident.Initialize` (`src/Game/Game.Prefabs/TrafficAccident.cs:30-39`). So the technique is `prefabs-and-assets`' — a system in the prefab-initialisation phase that queries the data component and overwrites it — and **not** patching, because the value the game reads is the component, never the class field. The field-initializer ruling above is the reason a reader must be told this: the obvious move of Harmony-patching `CarPathfind.Initialize` and changing a literal is both harder and wrong, since the asset overrides the literal anyway.

**Changing routing behaviour means changing weights or rules at the request site, and every request site is a system.** The weight profiles are literals inside `OnUpdate`-scheduled jobs (`RandomTrafficDispatchSystem.cs:221`, `FireRescueDispatchSystem.cs:405`, and the rest of the census above), so reaching them is `patching`'s — a transpiler or a prefix on the job-scheduling method — with `mod-lifecycle-and-ordering` deciding when the patch is applied. The cheaper alternative, and the one worth teaching first, is `ecs-in-this-game`': write `RuleFlags` and `CarLaneFlags` onto the lanes themselves and let the vanilla weights do the work.

**Changing the network means adding a component and letting the pipeline run.** `PathfindUpdated`, `Updated` on a lane, and `Updated` on an edge are three triggers of increasing cost (the finding above), and adding one is an `EntityCommandBuffer` write — `ecs-in-this-game`'s territory — ordered by `mod-lifecycle-and-ordering` into the right `Modification*` phase. Two traps the reference owes, both corroborated by the corpus: **the marker does not always survive being added in the phase that made the change** (`Anarchy` defers it to the next frame through its own tag, `Anarchy/Systems/Common/HandleUpdateNextFrameSystem.cs:74-77`), and **the fan-out has to reach further than the entity you edited** — node, every connected edge, and each of those edges' *far* node, because lane generation at a node depends on the composition of edges whose other end may have changed (`Traffic/Code/Systems/Serialization/TrafficDataMigrationSystem.ValidateLoadedDataJob.cs:398-416`), plus `BatchesUpdated` and the road `Aggregated.m_Aggregate` when the change is geometric (`NodeController/Main/Tools/NodeControllerTool.EcsWrites.cs:58-87`).

**Replacing a network system is `patching`'s and `mod-lifecycle-and-ordering`'s jointly, and this topic supplies the cost figure.** The pattern both corpus forks use is `GetOrCreateSystemManaged<T>().Enabled = false` followed by `UpdateBefore<MyFork, T>(<the phase vanilla registers T at>)` — anchoring against the disabled system rather than at the phase, which is what preserves the ordering slot. What the reference owes beside it is the price: `NodeController`'s fork of `GeometrySystem` is 3,422 lines carrying one `lerp` turned into an assignment, and `Traffic`'s fork of `LaneSystem` is 9,659. And the reason substitution rather than coexistence: `LaneReferencesSystem.GetSkipLaneQueue()` allocates a fresh queue per call and `AddSkipLaneWriter` overwrites its dependency (`src/Game/Game.Net/LaneReferencesSystem.cs:753-762`), so two callers silently lose one queue and one dependency.

**Issuing a pathfind request is a supported extension point with a hard limit.** `PathfindSetupSystem.GetQueue(this, maxDelayFrames, spreadFrames)` is public and `GetSetupQuery` exposes the system's own `GetEntityQuery` (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/PathfindSetupSystem.cs:395-435`), so a mod system can enqueue `SetupQueueItem`s exactly as vanilla does. The limit is `SetupTargetType`'s hardcoded switch (`:454-556`): a mod may not add a target type. `custom-tools` and `mod-lifecycle-and-ordering` own the registration; the cost discipline is this topic's, and it is the simulation-clock throttle above.

**Reading traffic state for a UI or an info view is a `localization`/UI-side job over components this topic names.** `Road.m_TrafficFlowDuration*`/`m_TrafficFlowDistance*` through `NetUtils.GetTrafficFlowSpeed`, `TrafficFlowSystem.cityAverageTrafficFlow`/`cityAverageTrafficVolume`, `CarLane.m_FlowOffset`, `Game.Net.Bottleneck` and `NetCondition.m_Wear` are the readable surface.

**Boundaries with sibling topics, stated so the reference does not cross them.**

- **`transportation-and-vehicles`** owns the outside connection as an object, every transit line laid over the network, and vehicle AI itself. This topic stops at how traffic *routes once it is on the network*: `RandomTrafficDispatchSystem`, `TrafficSpawnerAISystem` and `OutsideConnectionDelaySystem` are here because they are routing; `TransportLineSystem`, `RoutePathSystem` and the `Game.Routes` half are not. Note the one hard dependency: `PathfindQueueSystem` reads `m_TransportLineSystem.GetMaxTransportSpeed(…)` every time it schedules worker jobs (`src/Game/Game.Pathfind/PathfindQueueSystem.cs:651`), so the transit network's top speed enters every pathfinding heuristic in the game.
- **`utilities-and-flow-networks`** owns the utility capacity a road carries. That sibling's research file has the ruled findings — water and sewage pipes have no capacity limit, and a composition-flag gate decides which roads carry which utility, with a highway carrying electricity only once it is lit. **This topic states none of it.** The wiki's `Roads` page claims "All road types aside from highways have a power line capacity of 40 MW (Low Voltage). Those that aren't bridges also carry water and sewage pipes" (https://cs2.paradoxwikis.com/Roads); the number is barred by the pre-launch balance ruling above, and the mechanism is the sibling's `NetCompositionHelpers.TestEdgeFlags` gate, already ruled. - **`citizens-and-households`** owns `Citizen.m_LeisureCounter`, `Household.m_ConsumptionPerDay` and `TripPriorityParametersData`'s priorities as balance; this topic owns only how they enter the weight and ceiling formulas. That file already records the same live trip-priority figures ("trip priorities 192 home / 96 work / 64 shopping against a base max cost of 8000"), read independently here and **matching**, so the two files agree at 1.6.0f1.

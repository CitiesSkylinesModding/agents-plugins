# Citizens and households

Verified against game version 1.6.0f1.

**Read this with the decompile open.**
Without one you cannot check anything below.
`cs2-modding-setup` provisions it.

The game models people as two entity kinds.
A citizen is one entity whose simulated state is mostly which components it carries right now — `Worker` means employed, `Student` enrolled, `TravelPurpose` busy — with no status field to switch on, so a query on a component is a query on a population.
A household is the container: it owns the money, the shopped goods, the members and the home, and wages, fees, consumption and the decision to move away all land on it rather than on any citizen.
The balance behind every rate and amount lives in parameter components on prefabs, so the map below routes each family of numbers to its owner.

## Traps

Stopping at the entry's traps misses those beside each sibling's listings.

**`GetArchetypeComponents` is not the whole archetype.**
The override's first line calls `base`, and `ArchetypePrefab.RefreshArchetype` then unions in every attached component plus `Created` and `Updated`, so a live citizen carries more than `CitizenPrefab`'s own list — `PrefabRef` among them.
Source: `src/Game/Game.Prefabs/CitizenPrefab.cs`, `src/Game/Game.Prefabs/ArchetypePrefab.cs`.

**The `Adult`/`Child`/`Teen`/`Elderly` tag components are a dead save-format representation.**
Their single consumer is a load-time migration that reads the tag, writes the equivalent through `Citizen.SetAge` and removes it, so a query on them matches nothing in a running city — read `Citizen.GetAge()`.
Source: `src/Game/Game.Citizens/Adult.cs`, `src/Game/Game.Serialization/RequiredComponentSystem.cs`.

**The three education bits are not a three-bit integer.**
`EducationBit3` alone means level 4 and levels 0–3 pack into the other two bits, so writing the bits directly produces levels the game never produces — call `SetEducationLevel`.
Source: `src/Game/Game.Citizens/Citizen.cs`, `src/Game/Game.Citizens/CitizenFlags.cs`.

**`HouseholdFlags.MovedIn` has one simulation writer, and it gates almost everything.**
Only a citizen arriving at the rented property as `ArriveType.Resident` sets it; happiness (tourists excepted), the population census, school application and full-price pathfind money weights all require it, so a mod that creates a household must drive an arrival or set the flag itself.
Source: `src/Game/Game.Simulation/CitizenTravelPurposeSystem.cs`, `src/Game/Game.Simulation/CountHouseholdDataSystem.cs`, `src/Game/Game.Simulation/CitizenHappinessSystem.cs`, `src/Game/Game.Simulation/ApplyToSchoolSystem.cs`, `src/Game/Game.Citizens/CitizenUtils.cs`.

**Overwriting `m_PseudoRandom` rerolls the citizen's whole personality.**
Every stable trait — sleep offset, study willingness, the death draw, partner type, car probability and the rest of the `CitizenPseudoRandom` seeds — derives from that one field.
Source: `src/Game/Game.Citizens/Citizen.cs`, `src/Game/Game.Citizens/CitizenPseudoRandom.cs`.

**The parameter components above are not all write-once.**
`GameModeSystem` has the loaded game mode rebuild parameter components on every load: a class under `src/Game/Game.Prefabs.Modes` naming yours makes it a candidate, and whether the loaded mode runs that class is authored asset data no code read settles.
A retune lands in `OnGameLoaded`, which fires after the whole `Deserialize` phase the pass runs in (`mod-lifecycle-and-ordering` carries the hook).
Source: `src/Game/Game.Prefabs.Modes/GameModeSystem.cs`, `src/Game/Game.Prefabs.Modes/EconomyParametersMode.cs`.

**A component query and the census count different homeless populations.**
The census books a `MovingAway` chunk as moving away before counting it homeless, excludes `Deleted` and `Temp`, publishes the previous pass's snapshot, and applies its moved-in filter to citizens rather than households — the game's own UI reports the census, so a mod counting the component reports a different number than the panel.
Source: `src/Game/Game.Simulation/CountHouseholdDataSystem.cs`, `src/Game/Game.Simulation/HouseholdBehaviorSystem.cs`.

**Rates.**
A system's passes per day is `262144 / GetUpdateInterval(phase)` — the interval, never a `kUpdatesPerDay` constant a listing names (`ecs-in-this-game` owns the two conventions relating them).
Consuming `UpdateFrame` divides the per-entity rate by the family's bucket count — sixteen for every system in this topic.

**The household tick** (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/HouseholdBehaviorSystem.cs`).
While goods last, the household burns `GetConsumptionMultiplier(m_ResourceConsumptionMultiplier, wealth) * m_ResourceConsumptionPerCitizen * members` per pass; at zero it raises a `HouseholdNeed` and shops, gated on `kMinimumShoppingMoney = 1000` spendable.
Moving away has three triggers, evaluated in order: `NoAdults`, `NotHappy` (the curve is in [happiness.md](happiness.md)), and `NoMoney` when `wealth + dailyIncome < -1000`.
A moved-in household losing its `PropertyRenter` gains `HomelessHousehold`, loses one money per pass instead of consuming, and its property-seek roll's denominator is divided by `CitizenParametersData.m_LookForHomeHomelessDivisor`.

**Tourists and commuters** are whole households, spawned at outside connections; their citizens carry the matching `CitizenFlags` bit and fail `CitizenUtils.IsWorkableCitizen` (a census predicate, not a hiring gate — commuters do get hired) and the resident predicates.

**The census** (`src/Game/Game.Simulation/CountHouseholdDataSystem.cs`).
`GetHouseholdCountData()` is the cheapest population read in the topic — household, citizen, age, education, worker, student, homeless and happiness totals in one call — but it is a managed method, unreachable from inside a Bursted job.
`GetResourceNeeds` and `GetEmployables` hand out arrays with the write `JobHandle` — combine it into your schedule and register back through `AddHouseholdDataReader`, or the next result job overwrites under it.
The same system maintains `CitizenFlags.ValidCitizen` on citizens of moved-in households and `Homeless` on every homeless household's members; its workplace sibling `CountWorkplacesSystem` is what `CitizenFindJobSystem` gates its job seeking on, while the census's own employables feed `FindJobSystem`, `CommuterSpawnSystem` and `IndustrialDemandSystem` among their consumers — a mod changing who counts as employable moves hiring, commuter spawning and industrial and office demand together.

## Bridges

- `ecs-in-this-game` — the `GetSingleton<T>` read behind every parameter row above, and the `UpdateFrame` bucket material the rates rule needs: consuming takes two forms, and a reader who assumes one misreads systems using the other.
- `mod-lifecycle-and-ordering` — registering or forking any system above; a substitute must reproduce the vanilla interval and `UpdateFrame` partitioning both, or it runs sixteen times too often or too rarely, silently.
- `performance-and-memory` — the reader/writer handle protocol behind the census arrays: take, combine, register back.
- `simulation-time-and-units` — 262144 frames per day, `TimeSystem.GetDay`, and `TimeSettingsData.m_DaysPerYear`, the divisor under every age threshold.
- `save-serialization` — the version and format gates on these components; `Citizen` serializes its packed `m_State` as one `short`, so a new `CitizenFlags` bit spends serialized space, and `HouseholdCitizen` is `IEmptySerializable`, rebuilt on load from the members' `HouseholdMember` back-references.
- `zoning-buildings-and-land-value` — the other side of `PropertyRenter`: this topic decides that a household rents, moves in and leaves; the building, its lot and its residential capacity are that topic's, and they feed the apartment happiness factor.
- `city-services-and-coverage` — the coverage numbers happiness consumes, school capacity and building efficiency.
- `economy-and-companies` — the other end of employment: `Employee`, `FreeWorkplaces`, production, and the company-side wage debit.
